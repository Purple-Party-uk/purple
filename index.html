Can Britain afford this? Yes, if Britain can make and do it.

{% include image.html caption="Builders on a housing site" ratio="4/3" %}

The government issues the pound, so it cannot run out of pounds the way a household can. It can run out of workers, materials and energy. If it spends beyond those, prices rise.

Our plans are built around that limit. We spend first on what lets Britain make more: homes, skills and research. We phase each change in over several years. And we write an inflation brake into law, checked by an independent referee, so nobody has to take a minister’s word for it.

Britain already borrows £132 billion a year. Our full costings will be published policy by policy.

Read how we pay for it

Rent today buys your landlord a house. Under Purple, it buys you one.

Almost every rented home becomes rent-to-buy. Each payment buys you a share, and within 25 years the home is yours. The Debt Jubilee pays off up to £100,000 of it on day one. Your payments never rise faster than wages, and your share comes with you when you move.

No more 20% VAT at the till. And most businesses stop collecting it.

Clothes, phone bills, petrol, repairs, haircuts: everyday spending comes out of VAT altogether, and the law makes sure shops pass the cut on. VAT stays only on luxuries and on things like gambling, tobacco and flights.

For businesses, that means no registration and no quarterly returns, unless you sell luxury goods, gambling, payday loans or flights. Pubs and corner shops are spared too: the tax on alcohol and tobacco is paid by the producer.

No more TV licence, either. The BBC is funded from general taxation, so no household pays a flat £180 a year whatever its income.
