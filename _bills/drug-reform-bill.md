key: drug-reform-bill
title: "Drug Law Reform Bill"
summary: "Regulates drugs by the harm they do and ends penalties for personal possession."
long_title: "A Bill to establish a harm-based framework for the regulation of psychoactive substances across three bands; to create the Substance Regulation Authority; to abolish criminal offences relating to personal possession of regulated substances; to create offences relating to unlicensed supply and production; to permit home cultivation of Band 1 substances; to establish a quality mark scheme for online retailers of Band 1 substances; to reform the regulation of alcohol including provision for minimum unit pricing by statutory instrument on recommendation of the Authority; to prohibit advertising of regulated substances including alcohol, tobacco, vapes, nicotine pouches, and other products designated by the Authority; to require plain packaging of tobacco, vapes, nicotine pouches, and Authority-designated products; to create criminal liability for persons who produce or procure prohibited advertising; to provide for the automatic expungement of historical possession convictions; to make provision for integrated health and treatment services throughout the United Kingdom; and for connected purposes."
policies: []
parts:
  - title: "The Substance Regulation Authority"
    clauses:
      - num: "1"
        title: "Establishment of the Substance Regulation Authority"
        text: |
          (1) There is established a body corporate to be known as the Substance Regulation Authority (in this Act referred to as "the Authority").

          (2) The Authority is an independent statutory body sponsored by the Secretary of State for Health.

          (3) The Authority is not a Crown body and its members and staff are not Crown servants.

          (4) The Authority has perpetual succession and a common seal and may sue and be sued in its own name.
      - num: "2"
        title: "Functions of the Authority"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority has the following functions—

          - (a) to classify psychoactive substances into bands in accordance with section 5;
          - (b) to grant, vary, suspend, and revoke licences for regulated premises and supervised consumption rooms under Part 2;
          - (c) to grant and withdraw quality marks for online retailers under section 11;
          - (d) to set and enforce product standards and testing requirements under section 19;
          - (e) to set purchase and quantity limits under section 17;
          - (f) to make annual recommendations on the minimum unit price for alcohol under section 30;
          - (g) to monitor the operation of the regulated market and publish annual reports to Parliament;
          - (h) to advise the Secretary of State on substance-related public health matters;
          - (i) to carry out such other functions as are conferred on it by or under this Act.

          (2) In exercising its functions the Authority must have regard to—

          - (a) the evidence on the harms caused by substances to individuals and communities;
          - (b) the public health benefits of regulated legal supply over criminal markets;
          - (c) the need to eliminate or reduce criminal supply of psychoactive substances;
          - (d) the principle that persons who use substances should have access to safe, quality-assured products regardless of their engagement with treatment services.
      - num: "3"
        title: "Membership and governance"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority shall consist of a Chair, between six and ten other members, and a Director-General, each appointed through the process in subsection (2).

          (2) The appointment process is as follows—

          - (a) an Independent Nominations Panel produces a shortlist of candidates based on merit, relevant expertise, and independence from political or commercial interests;
          - (b) a citizens' jury of between 15 and 20 persons drawn by stratified random selection scrutinises the shortlisted candidates;
          - (c) the citizens' jury's decision to approve or reject each candidate is binding;
          - (d) the Secretary of State has no role in shortlisting or approval.

          (3) Members must include persons with expertise in pharmacology and toxicology, public health and epidemiology, addiction medicine and treatment, criminal justice, and lived experience of substance use. At least one member must have lived experience of substance use or addiction.

          (4) The Authority must publish the criteria used in band classification decisions, minutes of all board meetings, annual accounts audited by the Comptroller and Auditor General, and annual reports on the operation of the regulated market.

          (5) The Authority is subject to the Freedom of Information Act 2000.
      - num: "4"
        title: "Staff and pay"
        text: |
          (1) Staff of the Authority are employed by the Authority on terms and conditions determined by the Authority.

          (2) The remuneration of all staff of the Authority, including the Director-General, is governed by the civil service pay spine and associated pay regulations as they apply from time to time.
      - num: "5"
        title: "Classification of substances into bands"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority must classify each psychoactive substance into one of the following bands—

          - (a) Band 1 — lower and moderate harm substances;
          - (b) Band 2 — higher harm substances;
          - (c) Band 3 — severe harm substances.

          (2) The bands determine the regulatory environment within which a substance may be supplied. Take-home supply is permitted across all bands in accordance with Part 2.

          (3) In assessing harm the Authority must consider the physical harm the substance causes to individual users, the risk of dependence and addiction, the harm to persons other than the user, the harm caused by patterns of use at population level, and evidence from jurisdictions where the substance is legally regulated.

          (4) The initial band classifications are set out in Schedule 1.

          (5) The Authority may at any time review and reclassify any substance and must review all band classifications at least once every three years.
      - num: "6"
        title: "Procedure for band classification and reclassification"
        text: |
          (1) Before classifying or reclassifying a substance the Authority must commission or consider the available evidence on the substance's harms, consult persons with relevant expertise, publish a draft classification and invite representations, and consider any representations received.

          (2) Where the Authority considers that a substance poses an imminent and serious risk to public health it may issue an emergency classification taking effect immediately, subject to review within 90 days. An emergency classification must be laid before Parliament within 7 days.

          (3) Classifications take effect no earlier than 28 days after publication, except emergency classifications under subsection (2).
      - num: "7"
        title: "Publication of band classifications"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority must maintain and publish a freely accessible register of all substances and their current band classification, updated within 7 days of any reclassification.
  - title: "The Three-Band Regulatory Framework"
    clauses:
      - num: "8"
        title: "The core principle: take-home permitted across all bands"
        text: |
          (1) A person who has lawfully obtained a banded substance may take it away from the premises of supply and consume it elsewhere.

          (2) Take-home supply is permitted across all three bands, subject to the quantity limits set under section 17 and the supply conditions applicable to each band.

          (3) What distinguishes the bands is not whether take-home is permitted but the quality assurance requirements applicable to the substance, the qualifications and regulatory requirements applying to the person who supplies it, the purchase and quantity limits applying to each transaction, the nature and intensity of the support offer made at the point of supply, and the availability of on-site supervised consumption facilities.

          (4) The intensity of quality assurance, professional oversight, and support infrastructure increases from Band 1 to Band 3. The freedom of the individual to take the substance home and make their own choices about its consumption does not diminish.
      - num: "9"
        title: "Band 1 substances — lower and moderate harm"
        text: |
          (1) A Band 1 substance may be supplied—

          - (a) from premises holding a Band 1 specialist shop licence granted by the Authority, to a person aged 18 or over;
          - (b) by an online retailer holding a Band 1 quality mark granted by the Authority under section 11, to a person aged 18 or over; or
          - (c) through home cultivation in accordance with section 10.

          (2) Band 1 specialist shop licensed premises may provide a designated area for on-site consumption. Use of that area is voluntary.

          (3) Band 1 substances may be taken home from licensed premises or delivered to the home by an online retailer by the purchaser.

          (4) The supply conditions applicable to Band 1 licensed premises are set out in Schedule 2, Part 1.

          (5) Band 1 substances include cannabis, psilocybin, ketamine, MDMA, LSD, mescaline, khat, and nitrous oxide as set out in Schedule 1, subject to reclassification by the Authority.
      - num: "10"
        title: "Home cultivation of Band 1 substances"
        text: |
          (1) A person aged 18 or over may cultivate Band 1 substances at their home for personal use without a licence, subject to the limits in this section.

          (2) The limit for home cultivation of cannabis is four plants per household.

          (3) The Authority may by notice set home cultivation limits for other Band 1 substances where home cultivation is practicable.

          (4) Home cultivation under this section is permitted for personal use only and does not authorise supply to any other person.

          (5) Home cultivation is not subject to the product testing requirements in section 19, which apply to commercially supplied products only. The Authority must publish guidance on safe home cultivation practices.

          (6) A person who cultivates Band 1 substances in excess of the limits in this section commits an offence under section 23.
      - num: "11"
        title: "Online sale of Band 1 substances and the quality mark scheme"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority must establish and administer a quality mark scheme for online retailers of Band 1 substances.

          (2) An online retailer may apply to the Authority for a quality mark. The Authority may grant a quality mark where satisfied that the applicant meets the conditions in subsection (3).

          (3) The conditions for holding a quality mark are—

          - (a) all products are tested by an Authority-approved laboratory and test results are published on the retailer's website for each product batch;
          - (b) products meet the product standards set by the Authority under section 19;
          - (c) age verification is carried out at the point of sale and at the point of delivery in accordance with requirements set by the Authority;
          - (d) delivery is made only to the named purchaser or a person aged 18 or over at the delivery address who confirms their age;
          - (e) the quality mark is displayed prominently on the retailer's website and on all product packaging;
          - (f) harm reduction information approved by the Authority accompanies every order;
          - (g) the retailer maintains records of all sales and makes them available to the Authority on request;
          - (h) no advertising is placed by the retailer in breach of Part 5 of this Act.

          (4) The Authority may withdraw a quality mark where a retailer fails to comply with the conditions in subsection (3).

          (5) A retailer who sells Band 1 substances online without holding a quality mark commits an offence under section 22.

          (6) Online sale of Band 2 or Band 3 substances is not permitted under any circumstances.
      - num: "12"
        title: "Band 2 substances — higher harm"
        text: |
          (1) A Band 2 substance may only be supplied by a registered pharmacist from a pharmacy counter, to a person aged 18 or over who has provided valid photographic identification.

          (2) A pharmacist supplying a Band 2 substance must—

          - (a) provide the person with a standardised harm information document in a form approved by the Authority;
          - (b) make a non-judgmental verbal offer of information about Band 3 services and Integrated Health Centres;
          - (c) where requested, provide a referral to an Integrated Health Centre or to a Band 3 dispensing point.

          (3) No register of purchasers shall be kept. A pharmacist must not record the identity of persons who purchase Band 2 substances or share any such information with police, courts, or other agencies.

          (4) Band 2 substances may be taken home by the purchaser.

          (5) A pharmacist is not required to supply a Band 2 substance if they have reasonable grounds to believe the person intends to supply it to another person.

          (6) The supply conditions applicable to Band 2 are set out in Schedule 2, Part 2.

          (7) Band 2 substances include cocaine hydrochloride, amphetamine sulphate, methamphetamine, synthetic cannabinoids, and non-prescription benzodiazepines as set out in Schedule 1, subject to reclassification by the Authority.
      - num: "13"
        title: "Band 3 substances — severe harm"
        text: |
          (1) A Band 3 substance may only be dispensed at a licensed Band 3 dispensing point operated as part of or in conjunction with an Integrated Health Centre, by or under the supervision of a registered healthcare professional.

          (2) A person receiving a Band 3 substance must be offered, at the time of dispensing—

          - (a) access to a supervised consumption room if one is available at those premises;
          - (b) information about treatment and support services available at the Integrated Health Centre;
          - (c) any other support services available on-site.

          (3) The offer under subsection (2) must be made in a non-coercive manner. A person may decline any or all elements of the offer without affecting their entitlement to receive the substance.

          (4) Band 3 substances may be taken home by the recipient. Take-home supply does not require the person to engage with treatment or to be assessed as meeting any clinical threshold — it is available to all recipients.

          (5) Clinical staff may, where a person consents, provide a structured take-home supply plan setting quantities and intervals. A person who declines such a plan continues to be entitled to take-home supply on each attendance.

          (6) The supply conditions applicable to Band 3 dispensing are set out in Schedule 2, Part 3.

          (7) Band 3 substances include heroin, crack cocaine, fentanyl, and fentanyl analogues as set out in Schedule 1, subject to reclassification by the Authority.
      - num: "14"
        title: "Prohibition on sale in general retail premises"
        text: |
          (1) No banded substance may be sold from any general retail premises including supermarkets, convenience stores, petrol stations, department stores, or any other premises whose primary business is general retail.

          (2) Subsection (1) does not apply to a pharmacy counter that is physically located within general retail premises, from which a registered pharmacist may supply Band 2 substances in accordance with section 12.

          (3) For the purposes of subsection (2), a pharmacy counter is a counter staffed by a registered pharmacist at which prescription and non-prescription medicines are dispensed, forming a distinct part of the premises that is separately staffed and supervised.

          (4) No Band 1 substance may be sold from a pharmacy counter within general retail premises. Band 1 substances may only be sold from specialist licensed premises or by licensed online retailers.

          (5) A person who contravenes this section commits an offence under section 26.
      - num: "15"
        title: "Licensing of regulated premises"
        text: |
          (1) A person who wishes to operate regulated premises must apply to the Authority for a licence of the appropriate band.

          (2) An application must include details of the premises, the applicant, a compliance plan, and such other information as the Authority may require.

          (3) The Authority may grant a licence subject to conditions, refuse an application, or grant a provisional licence pending inspection. The Authority must give reasons for any refusal.

          (4) A licence holder may appeal a refusal or conditions to the First-tier Tribunal (General Regulatory Chamber).
      - num: "16"
        title: "Conditions of licences"
        text: |
          (1) All licences are subject to the conditions set out in Schedule 2 applicable to the relevant band.

          (2) The Authority may impose additional conditions on any licence having regard to the circumstances of the particular premises and may suspend or revoke a licence where the licence holder has failed to comply with its conditions.
      - num: "17"
        title: "Purchase and quantity limits"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority must set purchase and quantity limits for each band specifying the maximum quantity of a substance that may be supplied to a single person in any single transaction or period, having regard to personal use quantities, the harm profile of the substance, and the need to prevent onward supply.

          (2) For Band 3, quantity limits for take-home supply must also have regard to safety in the event of accidental consumption by a third party.
      - num: "18"
        title: "Age restrictions"
        text: |
          (1) No banded substance may be supplied to a person under the age of 18.

          (2) A licence holder, quality mark holder, or pharmacist must take reasonable steps to verify the age of any person who appears to be under 25.

          (3) It is a defence to a charge under section 25 that the person took all reasonable steps to verify the recipient's age and reasonably believed they were 18 or over.
      - num: "19"
        title: "Product standards and testing requirements"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority must set product standards for commercially supplied substances in each band, including standards relating to purity, concentration, permitted additives, packaging, labelling, and maximum dose per unit.

          (2) All commercially supplied substances must be tested by an Authority-approved laboratory before supply. A licence holder or quality mark holder must maintain records of all product testing and make them available to the Authority on request.

          (3) Product testing requirements under this section do not apply to substances cultivated at home under section 10.
  - title: "Criminal Offences"
    clauses:
      - num: "20"
        title: "Abolition of possession offences"
        text: |
          (1) It is not an offence under this Act or any other enactment for a person to possess a substance classified in Schedule 1 to this Act, regardless of the quantity possessed.

          (2) Sections 5(1) and 5(2) of the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 do not apply to any substance classified under this Act.

          (3) Nothing in this section affects the offences created by sections 22 to 26 of this Act.
      - num: "21"
        title: "Repeal of Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 supply and production offences for banded substances"
        text: |
          (1) Sections 4(1) and 4(3) of the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 do not apply to any substance classified under this Act.

          (2) The supply and production of banded substances is governed solely by the offences in sections 22 to 26 of this Act.

          (3) Schedule 4 makes consequential amendments to the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971.

          (4) The Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 continues in full force in relation to any controlled drug that has not been classified under this Act.
      - num: "22"
        title: "Offence of unlicensed supply"
        text: |
          (1) A person commits an offence if they supply a banded substance without holding a licence or quality mark authorising that supply.

          (2) A person commits an offence if they sell a Band 1 substance online without holding a quality mark under section 11.

          (3) A person commits an offence if they supply a Band 2 substance otherwise than at a pharmacy counter.

          (4) A person commits an offence if they supply a Band 3 substance otherwise than from a licensed Band 3 dispensing point.

          (5) A person commits an offence if they supply a Band 2 or Band 3 substance online in any circumstances.

          (6) It is a defence that the person did not know and had no reason to suspect that the substance was a banded substance.
      - num: "23"
        title: "Offence of unlicensed production"
        text: |
          (1) A person commits an offence if they produce a banded substance without holding a production licence granted by the Authority.

          (2) Subsection (1) does not apply to the home cultivation of Band 1 substances within the limits set by section 10.

          (3) Cultivation of Band 1 substances in excess of the limits set by section 10 is an offence under this section.
      - num: "24"
        title: "Offence of supply of adulterated substances"
        text: |
          (1) A person commits an offence if they supply a banded substance that does not meet the product standards set by the Authority under section 19.

          (2) A person commits an offence if they supply a substance which they represent to be a banded substance but which contains any adulterant that poses a risk to the health of the recipient.
      - num: "25"
        title: "Offence of supply to persons under 18"
        text: |
          (1) A person commits an offence if they supply a banded substance to a person under the age of 18.

          (2) It is a defence that the person took all reasonable steps to verify the recipient's age and reasonably believed they were aged 18 or over.
      - num: "26"
        title: "Offence of operating unlicensed premises"
        text: |
          (1) A person commits an offence if they operate premises for the supply of banded substances without holding the appropriate licence.

          (2) A person commits an offence if they permit premises owned or controlled by them to be used for the unlicensed supply of banded substances.

          (3) A person commits an offence if they sell banded substances from general retail premises in contravention of section 14.
      - num: "27"
        title: "Penalties"
        text: |
          (1) The penalties for offences under this Part are set out in Schedule 3.

          (2) In determining the appropriate penalty a court must have regard to the scale of the unlicensed activity, the harm or risk caused, whether the offence was committed for commercial gain, and whether violence or the threat of violence was used.
  - title: "Alcohol Regulation"
    clauses:
      - num: "28"
        title: "Alcohol as a regulated substance"
        text: |
          (1) Alcohol is not classified within the banding system established by Part 2 of this Act.

          (2) The provisions of this Part apply to alcohol in addition to the existing licensing regime under the Licensing Act 2003.

          (3) The harm-reduction principles underlying the banding system apply to alcohol and are given effect by the provisions of this Part.
      - num: "29"
        title: "Minimum unit pricing by statutory instrument"
        text: |
          (1) The Secretary of State must by statutory instrument set the minimum unit price for alcohol in England and Wales.

          (2) No alcohol may be sold at a price per unit of alcohol below the minimum unit price for the time being in force.

          (3) The initial minimum unit price, to be set by statutory instrument within 6 months of Royal Assent, must not be less than 65 pence per unit.

          (4) A statutory instrument under this section is subject to the affirmative procedure.

          (5) In this section "unit" means 10 millilitres of pure alcohol.

          (6) A person who sells alcohol below the minimum unit price commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding level 5 on the standard scale.
      - num: "30"
        title: "Annual recommendation by the Authority"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority must by 1st November each year publish a recommendation to the Secretary of State on whether the minimum unit price should be varied for the following year, having regard to the Consumer Prices Index, evidence on the public health impact of the current price, evidence on consumption patterns and alcohol-related harm, and the impact on the licensed on-trade sector.

          (2) The Authority must publish the evidence base for its recommendation.

          (3) The Secretary of State must within 60 days of receiving the recommendation either lay a statutory instrument giving effect to it or lay a statement before Parliament under section 31.
      - num: "31"
        title: "Parliamentary rejection of recommendation"
        text: |
          (1) Where the Secretary of State does not intend to give effect to the Authority's recommendation, the Secretary of State must lay before Parliament a written statement setting out the Authority's recommendation, the reasons for not accepting it, and what minimum unit price the Secretary of State intends to set instead and why.

          (2) The existing minimum unit price continues in force until a new statutory instrument is made. The Secretary of State must lay a statutory instrument within 30 days of laying the statement under subsection (1).
      - num: "32"
        title: "Prohibition on below-cost selling"
        text: |
          (1) No alcohol may be sold at a price below the cost at which the seller acquired it. "Cost" means the wholesale acquisition price excluding value added tax.

          (2) A person who contravenes this section commits an offence and is liable on conviction on indictment to an unlimited fine.
      - num: "33"
        title: "Prohibition on multi-buy promotions"
        text: |
          (1) No person may offer, advertise, or provide any promotion that reduces the unit price of alcohol when purchased in greater quantity, links the purchase of alcohol to a discount on any other product, or provides any financial incentive for the purchase of a greater quantity of alcohol.

          (2) A person who contravenes this section commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to an unlimited fine.
      - num: "34"
        title: "Restrictions on retail placement"
        text: |
          (1) Alcohol may be sold in supermarkets and general retail premises only where the alcohol is located in a dedicated section clearly separate from other goods and not at or near the entrance, checkouts, or high-footfall areas; no alcohol is displayed or offered for sale outside the dedicated section; and all alcohol purchases are processed by a member of staff and may not be completed through a self-service checkout.

          (2) No end-of-aisle, entrance, or promotional display of alcohol is permitted in any retail premises.

          (3) A person who contravenes this section commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding level 5 on the standard scale.
      - num: "35"
        title: "Mandatory health labelling"
        text: |
          (1) All alcoholic products sold in England and Wales must bear a label including in a legible and prominent form the number of units of alcohol in the product, the Chief Medical Officer's low-risk drinking guidelines, a warning that alcohol is harmful in pregnancy, and the address of the NHS support and treatment information service.

          (2) The Secretary of State may by regulations specify the form, size, and prominence of the required label.
  - title: "Advertising, Packaging and Designated Products"
    clauses:
      - num: "36"
        title: "Designated products"
        text: |
          (1) The Authority may by notice designate any product as a "designated product" for the purposes of this Part where it is satisfied that the product poses a risk of harm to health comparable to a banded substance or to alcohol or tobacco.

          (2) The following products are designated products from the commencement of this section—

          - (a) vapes and e-cigarettes of all kinds;
          - (b) nicotine pouches;
          - (c) heated tobacco products;
          - (d) any other nicotine delivery product that does not fall within the definition of a medicinal product.

          (3) The Authority must publish and maintain a register of designated products, updated within 7 days of any designation or removal.

          (4) Before designating a product the Authority must publish a draft designation and invite representations, consider those representations, and have regard to the evidence on the health harms caused by the product.
      - num: "37"
        title: "Prohibition on advertising of regulated substances"
        text: |
          (1) No person may publish, broadcast, or display any advertisement that promotes or encourages the use of any banded substance, advertises the availability of any banded substance for purchase, or promotes any premises at which a banded substance is available.

          (2) This section does not prohibit factual product information on a licensed premises' own website, the quality mark and product testing information required under section 11, harm reduction information provided by the Authority or NHS, or information required to be provided under this Act.
      - num: "38"
        title: "Prohibition on advertising of alcohol, tobacco, and designated products"
        text: |
          (1) No person may publish, broadcast, or display any advertisement that promotes or encourages the purchase or consumption of alcohol, tobacco, or any designated product.

          (2) This section extends the prohibitions under the Tobacco Advertising and Promotion Act 2002 to alcohol and all designated products and applies equivalent enforcement mechanisms.

          (3) This section applies to all media including television, radio, on-demand audio-visual services, online and social media, outdoor advertising, print publications, and cinema.

          (4) This section does not prohibit the display of a product's brand name and price at the point of sale within a licensed premises or licensed retail area.
      - num: "39"
        title: "Prohibition on sponsorship"
        text: |
          (1) No person may enter into a sponsorship arrangement in which the name, brand, or product of an alcohol, tobacco, banded substance, or designated product producer, retailer, or distributor is associated with any sporting event, team, or venue; any cultural, musical, or entertainment event or venue; any educational institution; any broadcast or online content; or any clothing or merchandise.

          (2) Existing sponsorship arrangements must be terminated within 12 months of commencement of this section.
      - num: "40"
        title: "Plain packaging of tobacco, vapes, nicotine pouches, and designated products"
        text: |
          (1) All tobacco products, vapes, nicotine pouches, and designated products sold in the United Kingdom must be sold in plain packaging.

          (2) Plain packaging means packaging that—

          - (a) bears no logo, brand imagery, colour, or graphic feature other than the brand name and product name in a standardised typeface and size specified by the Authority;
          - (b) is in the standardised colour and finish specified by the Authority;
          - (c) bears the health warnings and other information required by the Authority in the form, size, and prominence specified by the Authority;
          - (d) bears no promotional text, descriptors, or claims of any kind.

          (3) The Authority must by regulations specify the standardised typeface, colour, finish, and health warning requirements applicable to each category of product.

          (4) The regulations under subsection (3) must require health warnings to occupy at least 65% of the front and back of the packaging.

          (5) A person who sells a product in packaging that does not comply with this section commits an offence.

          (6) A manufacturer or importer who places on the market a product in packaging that does not comply with this section commits an offence.
      - num: "41"
        title: "Enforcement and criminal liability"
        text: |
          (1) The Advertising Standards Authority is responsible for enforcing sections 37, 38, and 39 in relation to non-broadcast media. Ofcom is responsible for broadcast and on-demand media.

          (2) A person commits an offence if they—

          - (a) publish, broadcast, or display an advertisement in contravention of sections 37 or 38;
          - (b) produce an advertisement knowing or having reasonable grounds to believe it will be published, broadcast, or displayed in contravention of sections 37 or 38;
          - (c) procure the production or placement of an advertisement knowing or having reasonable grounds to believe it will be published, broadcast, or displayed in contravention of sections 37 or 38;
          - (d) enter into a sponsorship arrangement in contravention of section 39.

          (3) For the purposes of subsection (2)(b) and (c), "procure" includes commissioning, contracting for, approving, or authorising the production or placement of an advertisement.

          (4) A person guilty of an offence under subsection (2)(a), (b), (c), or (d) who is an individual is liable—

          - (a) on summary conviction, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 12 months or a fine or both;
          - (b) on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years or a fine or both.

          (5) A body corporate guilty of an offence under this section is liable on conviction on indictment to a fine not exceeding 10% of its annual global turnover for the financial year preceding the conviction.

          (6) Where an offence under this section is committed by a body corporate with the consent or connivance of a director, chief executive, chief marketing officer, or other senior manager responsible for advertising or marketing, that individual is also guilty of the offence and liable to the penalties in subsection (4).

          (7) A person guilty of an offence under section 40(5) or 40(6) is liable on summary conviction to an unlimited fine and on conviction on indictment to an unlimited fine.

          (8) A body corporate guilty of an offence under section 40(5) or 40(6) is liable to a fine not exceeding 10% of its annual global turnover.
  - title: "Integrated Health and Treatment Services"
    clauses:
      - num: "42"
        title: "Duty to establish Integrated Health Centres and equivalent services"
        text: |
          (1) The Secretary of State must ensure that an Integrated Health Centre is available and accessible to all persons in England, established and operational within 24 months of the commencement of this section.

          (2) The Scottish Ministers, the Welsh Ministers, and the Northern Ireland Executive must each ensure that equivalent services are available and accessible to all persons in their respective territories within 24 months of the commencement of this section.

          (3) Integrated Health Centres and equivalent services must be accessible without referral and without an appointment for initial contact, and must be funded from the relevant health budget as part of the mental health and addiction services allocation.
      - num: "43"
        title: "Services to be provided at Integrated Health Centres"
        text: |

## Summary

1. The Drug Law Reform Act 2026 establishes a three-band harm-based regulatory framework for psychoactive substances, replacing the prohibitionist model of the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 for classified substances. It abolishes all possession offences immediately on Royal Assent. It permits home cultivation of Band 1 substances. It establishes an online quality mark scheme for Band 1 retailers. It prohibits the sale of all banded substances in general retail premises. It reforms alcohol regulation through minimum unit pricing set by statutory instrument on annual recommendation of the Substance Regulation Authority. It requires automatic expungement of all historical possession convictions.

## The three-band logic

{:start="2"}
2. The three-band structure replaces the four-band structure in earlier drafts. The consolidation of lower and moderate harm substances into a single Band 1 reflects that the regulatory rationale — specialist shop environment with trained staff — is the same for cannabis, psilocybin, ketamine, and MDMA alike. The earlier distinction between Band A (on-site consumption permitted) and Band B (dosing precision requires staff) was a weaker basis for a separate band than the clear step-change between Band 1 (specialist retail), Band 2 (pharmacy), and Band 3 (medical centre).
3. The three bands represent three genuinely different regulatory environments with different professional oversight levels: trained retail staff, registered pharmacist, registered healthcare professional. That is a coherent and defensible escalation.

## Take-home as a consistent right

{:start="4"}
4. Take-home supply is a right in all three bands. Section 8 establishes this as a named principle. The bands are a quality and support escalator — what increases with harm level is the richness of the clinical and support infrastructure around the person, not restrictions on their right to take a substance home. Band 3 heroin supply is not supervised consumption by another name. It is a system in which a person can access pharmaceutical-grade heroin, be offered comprehensive support, and take their supply home. The evidence base — principally from Switzerland and the Netherlands — shows that voluntary supported supply with take-home availability is more effective at reducing mortality and social harm than any coercive model.

## No general retail sales

{:start="5"}
5. Section 14 prohibits the sale of all banded substances in general retail premises. This is a bright-line rule with no exceptions for Band 1. The specialist shop environment — trained staff, harm information displayed, age verification culture, no competing retail incentives — is the harm-reduction mechanism for Band 1. A supermarket cannot replicate that environment. The only concession is for pharmacy counters physically located within supermarkets, which may supply Band 2 substances in the same way as any other pharmacy. Band 1 is not available from any pharmacy counter.

## Online sales — Band 1 only

{:start="6"}
6. Online sale is permitted for Band 1 only, through the quality mark scheme in section 11. The quality mark requires batch-level product testing published online, age verification at point of sale and delivery, and compliance with all advertising prohibitions. This is a lighter-touch framework than the specialist shop environment — there is no trained staff interaction at the point of sale. That is accepted on harm-reduction grounds: a legal, quality-marked online market for Band 1 substances is substantially less harmful than the unregulated online grey market it displaces. Band 2 and Band 3 cannot be sold online under any circumstances — the pharmacist and healthcare professional interactions are the regulatory mechanism, and those cannot be replicated online.

## No purchase register for Band 2

{:start="7"}
7. Section 12(3) explicitly prohibits pharmacists from keeping a register of Band 2 purchasers or sharing purchaser identity with any third party. This is a deliberate and important provision. A register of who buys cocaine from a pharmacy would be a surveillance mechanism that would deter use of the legal regulated market in favour of the criminal market — precisely the opposite of the intended effect. The harm-reduction rationale for regulated supply depends on people using the regulated system. Anything that deters use of the regulated system increases harm.

## Minimum unit pricing mechanism

{:start="8"}
8. Sections 29 to 31 separate the legislative framework from the specific price. The floor of 65 pence and the affirmative procedure for price-setting statutory instruments are in primary legislation. The annual price recommendation comes from the Authority, not ministers, and is based on published evidence. A Secretary of State who rejects the recommendation must explain why in Parliament and must promptly lay an alternative statutory instrument. This prevents the price from being used as a political instrument in either direction — neither suppressed to please the drinks industry nor ratcheted up beyond what evidence supports.

## Territorial extent and human rights

{:start="9"}
9. The Act extends to England and Wales. Extension to Scotland or Northern Ireland requires consent of the relevant devolved legislature. The Secretary of State has made a statement under section 19 of the Human Rights Act 1998 that the provisions are compatible with Convention rights. Article 8 (private life) supports decriminalisation and take-home. Article 2 (right to life) supports harm-reduction and Integrated Health Centre provisions.
