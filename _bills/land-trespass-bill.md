key: land-trespass-bill
title: Land Rights, Trespass and Vehicle Removal Bill
long_title: A Bill to make provision conferring powers on the police and local authorities to remove trespassers from residential and personal land; to create a graduated system of warnings, fixed penalty notices, and criminal offences in respect of trespass; to make provision about squatting in occupied and unoccupied buildings; to confer powers on local authorities to remove vehicles parked illegally on private land or obstructing private driveways; to provide for cost recovery and liability in respect of vehicle removal; and for connected purposes.

parts:
- title: Trespass on Personal and Residential Land
  clauses:
  - num: '1'
    title: Police power to direct trespassers to leave
    text: |
      (1) Where a constable reasonably believes that a person is trespassing on residential or personal land, the constable may direct that person to leave the land immediately.

      (2) A direction under subsection (1) may be given orally or in writing.

      (3) A person who fails to comply with a direction given under subsection (1) without reasonable excuse commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding level 2 on the standard scale.

      (4) In exercising the power under subsection (1), a constable must have regard to any code of practice issued under section 22.

      (5) The power under subsection (1) does not limit any other power of a constable to direct persons to leave land or premises.

      (6) For the purposes of this section, a person is trespassing on land if they are present on the land without the permission of the occupier or owner and without other lawful authority.
  - num: '2'
    title: Formal trespass warning
    text: |
      (1) Where a constable exercises the power under section 1 in respect of a person, the constable must—

      - (a) record the direction in the Trespass Warning Register established under section 5; and
      - (b) issue the person with a written notice (a "trespass warning") specifying—
        - (i) the date, time, and location of the trespass;
        - (ii) the identity of the land or property concerned;
        - (iii) the consequences of further trespass at that location.

      (2) A trespass warning issued under subsection (1) is the first stage of the graduated response under this Part.

      (3) A trespass warning does not constitute a criminal record and must not be treated as such for any purpose.

      (4) A trespass warning expires after 3 years from the date of issue if no further trespass warning or fixed penalty notice has been issued in respect of the same land during that period.
  - num: '3'
    title: Fixed penalty notice for repeat trespass
    text: |
      (1) Where a constable reasonably believes that a person has trespassed on residential or personal land on a second or subsequent occasion, and a trespass warning in respect of the same land has previously been issued to that person under section 2, the constable may issue a fixed penalty notice to that person.

      (2) A fixed penalty notice issued under this section must comply with Schedule 1.

      (3) The fixed penalty for—

      - (a) a second instance of trespass at the same location is £150;
      - (b) a third instance of trespass at the same location is £500.

      (4) Payment of a fixed penalty notice discharges liability for the offence to which it relates.

      (5) Where a person fails to pay a fixed penalty notice within 28 days of its issue, the constable or local authority may refer the matter for prosecution under section 4.
  - num: '4'
    title: Offence of persistent trespass
    text: |
      (1) A person commits an offence of persistent trespass if—

      - (a) they trespass on residential or personal land on three or more occasions;
      - (b) a trespass warning under section 2 has been issued in respect of that land; and
      - (c) at least one fixed penalty notice under section 3 has previously been issued to that person in respect of the same land.

      (2) A person guilty of an offence under this section is liable on summary conviction to—

      - (a) imprisonment for a term not exceeding 51 weeks; or
      - (b) a fine; or
      - (c) both.

      (3) In proceedings under this section, a certificate issued by the Secretary of State confirming the contents of the Trespass Warning Register in respect of the defendant is admissible as evidence of the matters stated in it.

      (4) It is a defence to a charge under this section for the defendant to show that—

      - (a) they had a reasonable belief that they had permission to be on the land; or
      - (b) they had other lawful authority to be on the land; or
      - (c) the trespass was necessary to prevent danger to life.
  - num: '5'
    title: Trespass Warning Register
    text: |
      (1) The Secretary of State must establish and maintain a national register (the "Trespass Warning Register") recording—

      - (a) the details of every trespass warning issued under section 2;
      - (b) the details of every fixed penalty notice issued under section 3;
      - (c) outcomes of any prosecution under section 4.

      (2) A record in the Trespass Warning Register must specify—

      - (a) the name and date of birth of the person warned or penalised;
      - (b) the address or description of the land concerned;
      - (c) the date of the warning, notice, or conviction;
      - (d) the stage of the graduated response reached;
      - (e) the police force responsible for the entry.

      (3) The Trespass Warning Register must be accessible to—

      - (a) any constable in England, Wales, Scotland, or Northern Ireland for the purposes of this Act;
      - (b) the Secretary of State for the purposes of administration and oversight;
      - (c) the individual named in a record, who may access their own record at any time through the GOV.UK One Login service or such successor government digital identity service as the Secretary of State may by regulations designate.

      (4) Information in the Trespass Warning Register—

      - (a) must not be disclosed except for the purposes of this Act or law enforcement;
      - (b) is not part of the person's criminal record unless a conviction under section 4 has been recorded;
      - (c) must be deleted in respect of a person upon expiry of the relevant trespass warning under section 2(4) where no fixed penalty notice or conviction has been recorded.

      (5) The Secretary of State may by regulations provide for the Trespass Warning Register to be integrated with the Police National Computer or any successor national law enforcement database.
  - num: '6'
    title: Defences
    text: |
      (1) It is a defence in any proceedings under this Part for a person to show that—

      - (a) they were exercising a public right of way over the land;
      - (b) they entered the land in the honest and reasonable belief that they had permission to do so;
      - (c) they entered the land in an emergency to prevent danger to life or serious injury;
      - (d) they had lawful authority under any other enactment to be on the land.

      (2) The burden of proof in respect of a defence under this section rests on the defendant on the balance of probabilities.
- title: Squatting
  clauses:
  - num: '7'
    title: Squatting in an occupied building
    text: |
      (1) A person commits an offence if—

      - (a) they enter or are present in a building as a trespasser; and
      - (b) the building is an occupied building within the meaning of subsection (3).

      (2) A person guilty of an offence under this section is liable on summary conviction to—

      - (a) imprisonment for a term not exceeding 51 weeks; or
      - (b) a fine not exceeding level 5 on the standard scale; or
      - (c) both.

      (3) For the purposes of this section a building is an "occupied building" if—

      - (a) it is someone's home or primary residence, whether or not they are present at the time of the trespass;
      - (b) it is being actively used as business premises; or
      - (c) it is being actively used for any other lawful purpose on a regular basis.

      (4) Where a constable reasonably believes that a person has committed or is committing an offence under this section, the constable may—

      - (a) enter the building to arrest that person without a warrant; and
      - (b) remove that person from the building.

      (5) For the purposes of subsection (4), the power to enter the building may be exercised using reasonable force if necessary.

      (6) The occupier or owner of the building must be informed of any removal under subsection (4) as soon as reasonably practicable.
  - num: '8'
    title: Right to remain in unoccupied buildings
    text: |
      (1) Where a building has been unoccupied for a continuous period of at least 18 months, a person present in that building as a trespasser (a "qualifying occupant") has the right to remain in the building unless the owner satisfies the court that at least one of the conditions in subsection (3) is met.

      (2) The 18-month period under subsection (1) must be continuous and immediately preceding the date on which the qualifying occupant first occupied the building.

      (3) The conditions referred to in subsection (1) are that the owner—

      - (a) has made a valid application for planning permission in respect of the building that is actively being pursued and has not lapsed;
      - (b) has entered into a signed building contract with a contractor for works to the building dated within the preceding 6 months;
      - (c) has the building actively marketed for sale or let with a registered agent, evidenced by a current listing dated within the preceding 3 months; or
      - (d) has registered the building with the local authority's empty homes programme with an agreed and realistic return-to-use date falling within the next 12 months.

      (4) In satisfying a condition under subsection (3) the owner must demonstrate genuine and active intent — it is not sufficient to produce documentation that is stale, lapsed, or created for the purpose of defeating the qualifying occupant's right to remain.

      (5) Where the owner cannot satisfy any condition under subsection (3), the court must confirm the qualifying occupant's right to remain and record that confirmation in the register maintained under section 10.

      (6) Where the owner satisfies a condition under subsection (3), the court may make a possession order requiring the qualifying occupant to vacate within 7 days.

      (7) Schedule 2 makes further provision about the procedure under this section.
  - num: '9'
    title: Reoccupation by owner
    text: |
      (1) An owner whose building is occupied by a qualifying occupant under section 8 may apply to the court for a reoccupation order where the owner intends to reoccupy the building themselves.

      (2) The court must grant a reoccupation order where satisfied that—

      - (a) the owner genuinely intends to reoccupy the building as their primary residence or for active business use; and
      - (b) such reoccupation will commence within 60 days of the order being made; and
      - (c) the owner can demonstrate that the reoccupation is not for the purpose of defeating the qualifying occupant's rights under this Part.

      (3) In determining whether reoccupation is genuine under subsection (2), the court must have regard to—

      - (a) the owner's history of occupation and use of the building;
      - (b) any pattern of repeated short-term reoccupation;
      - (c) whether the owner has made material preparations for reoccupation;
      - (d) any other circumstances the court considers relevant.

      (4) Where a reoccupation order is granted—

      - (a) the qualifying occupant must vacate within 14 days of the order;
      - (b) the owner must reoccupy the building within 60 days of the order;
      - (c) the owner must maintain continuous occupation for a minimum period of 12 months.

      (5) The period of reoccupation under subsection (4)(c) does not count towards the qualifying occupant's five-year entitlement period under section 10.

      (6) Where an owner vacates the building within 24 months of the end of the minimum reoccupation period under subsection (4)(c), a qualifying occupant may apply to the court for a finding that the reoccupation was not genuine.

      (7) In determining an application under subsection (6), the court must consider whether, on the balance of probabilities, the reoccupation was primarily for the purpose of defeating the qualifying occupant's rights rather than for genuine residential or business use.

      (8) Where the court makes a finding under subsection (7) that reoccupation was not genuine—

      - (a) the qualifying occupant's five-year entitlement period is treated as having been continuously accrued throughout the period of reoccupation; and
      - (b) the court may make an immediate ownership registration order under section 10(4) where the five-year period has been reached.

      (9) The 18-month initial empty period under section 8(1) does not count towards the qualifying occupant's five-year entitlement period under section 10.
  - num: '10'
    title: Ownership registration after five years
    text: |
      (1) Where a qualifying occupant has been in continuous occupation of a building under section 8 for a period of five years from the date the court confirmed their right to remain under section 8(5), the qualifying occupant may apply to His Majesty's Land Registry for registration as the owner of the building.

      (2) For the purposes of subsection (1), continuous occupation means unbroken physical occupation of the building as the qualifying occupant's primary residence or for active use, subject to reasonable absences not exceeding 3 months in aggregate in any 12-month period.

      (3) An application under subsection (1) must be accompanied by—

      - (a) a copy of the court confirmation under section 8(5);
      - (b) evidence of continuous occupation for the five-year period;
      - (c) a statutory declaration by the qualifying occupant confirming the matters in subsection (2).

      (4) Upon receipt of a valid application under subsection (1), the Land Registry must—

      - (a) serve notice on the registered owner of the building;
      - (b) allow the registered owner 65 days in which to object to the application.

      (5) The registered owner may object to an application under subsection (1) only on the following grounds—

      - (a) the qualifying occupant has not been in continuous occupation for five years;
      - (b) the qualifying occupant has abandoned occupation during the five-year period;
      - (c) the owner can demonstrate reasonable excuse for the building having remained empty throughout the qualifying period.

      (6) For the purposes of subsection (5)(c), reasonable excuse includes—

      - (a) serious illness of the owner or a member of their immediate family preventing active management of the property;
      - (b) active probate or inheritance dispute preventing legal dealings with the property;
      - (c) financial incapacity demonstrated by insolvency proceedings;
      - (d) any other circumstance the court considers genuinely prevented the owner from bringing the building back into use.

      (7) A reasonable excuse defence under subsection (5)(c) may only be raised at the point the qualifying occupant makes their application for registration — it may not be used to interrupt or void the qualifying occupant's right to remain during the five-year period.

      (8) Where no valid objection is made within 65 days, or where an objection is rejected by the court, the Land Registry must register the qualifying occupant as the owner of the building with freehold title absolute.

      (9) Where ownership is transferred under this section—

      - (a) any mortgage or charge secured on the building is extinguished;
      - (b) the previous owner's interest in the building is extinguished;
      - (c) the new owner takes the building subject to any lawful easements or rights of way.

      (10) The provisions of this section do not apply to—

      - (a) any building that forms part of an actively farmed agricultural holding where any part of the holding is in productive agricultural use;
      - (b) any barn, outbuilding, or farm structure that is connected by ownership to land in active agricultural use, unless the entire holding including all land has been wholly derelict and unused for the qualifying period;
      - (c) any building owned by a local authority, registered social landlord, or other public body.

      (11) For the purposes of subsection (10)(b), a building is connected by ownership to land in active agricultural use where—

      - (a) it is registered under the same title as agricultural land; or
      - (b) it is within the curtilage of a working farm; or
      - (c) its removal from the holding would materially interfere with the agricultural operation.

      (12) Where an entire agricultural holding including all land and buildings has been wholly derelict and unused for the qualifying period, the right to remain under section 8 and the ownership registration right under this section apply to the entire holding including all land and buildings.
  - num: '11'
    title: Welfare consideration and housing referral
    text: |
      (1) Where a court is considering proceedings under section 8 and a qualifying occupant appears to be homeless or at risk of homelessness should they be required to vacate, the court must notify the local housing authority for the area in which the building is situated.

      (2) Upon notification under subsection (1), the local housing authority must—

      - (a) assess the housing needs of the qualifying occupant within 48 hours;
      - (b) provide the court with a report on that assessment before any possession order is made;
      - (c) where the qualifying occupant is found to be homeless or at risk of homelessness, make an offer of interim accommodation or support before removal is carried out.

      (3) Removal under a possession order must not be carried out until the local housing authority has complied with subsection (2)(c), unless—

      - (a) the qualifying occupant has refused the offer of interim accommodation or support; or
      - (b) the court is satisfied that delay would cause significant prejudice to the owner.
  - num: '12'
    title: Agricultural land — protection from trespass and squatting
    text: |
      (1) Nothing in this Part confers any right to remain on or occupy—

      - (a) agricultural land in active use;
      - (b) any barn, outbuilding, or farm structure connected by ownership to land in active agricultural use;
      - (c) any field, woodland, or other land forming part of a working agricultural holding.

      (2) Where a person is present on agricultural land or farm buildings forming part of a working agricultural holding as a trespasser, the owner or occupier may apply for immediate removal under the fast-track procedure in Schedule 2 regardless of how long the land or building has been unoccupied.

      (3) For the purposes of this section, an agricultural holding is "working" where any part of the land comprised in the holding is in productive agricultural use, including—

      - (a) arable cultivation;
      - (b) livestock grazing or rearing;
      - (c) horticulture or market gardening;
      - (d) forestry in active management;
      - (e) any other agricultural activity within the meaning of the Agriculture Act 1947.

      (4) The protections in this section apply whether or not individual buildings on the holding are in current use, provided that the holding as a whole is in active agricultural use.
  - num: '13'
    title: Penalties on conviction — squatting
    text: |
      (1) Where a person is convicted of an offence under section 7, the court may in addition to any other penalty—

      - (a) order the person to pay the reasonable costs of removal; and
      - (b) order the person to pay compensation to the owner or occupier for damage caused by the convicted person, where the owner can demonstrate on the balance of probabilities that the damage was caused by the convicted person and did not pre-exist their occupation.

      (2) A person who obstructs a constable or enforcement officer in the exercise of powers under this Part commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding level 3 on the standard scale.
- title: Vehicle Removal from Private Land
  clauses:
  - num: '14'
    title: Local authority power to remove vehicles
    text: |
      (1) A local authority may remove a vehicle from private land if—

      - (a) the vehicle is parked on private land without the permission of the owner or occupier of that land; or
      - (b) the vehicle is obstructing access to or egress from a private driveway or private access road.

      (2) The power under subsection (1) may be exercised by—

      - (a) an authorised officer of the local authority;
      - (b) a constable; or
      - (c) a contractor acting on behalf of the local authority or police.

      (3) In exercising the power under subsection (1), the local authority must comply with—

      - (a) the procedure in section 15; and
      - (b) any code of practice issued under section 22.

      (4) A vehicle removed under this section must be taken to a designated pound or storage facility maintained or approved by the local authority.
  - num: '15'
    title: Procedure for requesting removal
    text: |
      (1) The owner or occupier of private land may request the local authority or a constable to exercise the power under section 14 by—

      - (a) submitting a request in the form specified by the local authority or, in the case of a police request, by contacting the relevant police force; and
      - (b) providing such evidence as the local authority or constable may reasonably require to confirm that the vehicle is parked without permission or is obstructing access.

      (2) Upon receipt of a request under subsection (1)—

      - (a) an authorised officer of the local authority must attend the location—
        - (i) within 4 hours of receipt of the request, where the land is in an urban area; or
        - (ii) within 8 hours of receipt of the request, where the land is in a rural area; or
      - (b) where a constable has been requested and attends, the constable may exercise the power under section 14 immediately upon being satisfied that the vehicle is parked without permission or is obstructing access.

      (3) Before authorising removal, the authorised officer or constable must be satisfied that—

      - (a) the vehicle is parked without permission or is obstructing access; and
      - (b) removal is proportionate in the circumstances.

      (4) Where removal is authorised, the local authority or constable must make reasonable efforts to notify the registered keeper of the vehicle before removal is carried out, but the absence of such notification does not invalidate the removal.
  - num: '16'
    title: Cost recovery from registered keeper
    text: |
      (1) All costs reasonably incurred by a local authority in removing and storing a vehicle under section 14 are recoverable from the registered keeper of the vehicle.

      (2) The local authority must serve a notice on the registered keeper specifying—

      - (a) the amount of the costs recoverable;
      - (b) the address of the pound or storage facility where the vehicle is held;
      - (c) the period within which the keeper must pay and reclaim the vehicle.

      (3) The registered keeper must pay the costs and reclaim the vehicle within 28 days of the date of the notice under subsection (2).

      (4) Where the registered keeper fails to pay within the period specified in subsection (3), the local authority may—

      - (a) refer the debt for enforcement as a civil debt; and
      - (b) proceed to disposal under section 20.

      (5) Storage costs accrue from the date of removal until the date the vehicle is reclaimed or disposed of.

      (6) The Secretary of State may by regulations specify the maximum rates that a local authority may charge for removal and storage under this section.
  - num: '17'
    title: Liability for damage during removal
    text: |
      (1) Where a vehicle is removed under section 14 because it is parked without permission or is obstructing access, the registered keeper is liable for any damage caused to the vehicle during removal, unless the damage was caused by the negligence of the local authority or its contractor.

      (2) In proceedings relating to damage under this section, the burden of proving negligence on the part of the local authority or contractor rests on the registered keeper.

      (3) Liability under this section does not arise where the vehicle was lawfully parked at the time of removal.
  - num: '18'
    title: Emergency services removal
    text: |
      (1) Where a constable or a member of the fire and rescue service or ambulance service removes a vehicle in the course of an emergency response, and that vehicle was illegally parked or was obstructing an emergency response—

      - (a) the registered keeper is liable for any damage caused to the vehicle during removal; and
      - (b) the emergency service may recover the costs of removal from the registered keeper.

      (2) Subsection (1) does not apply where the vehicle was lawfully parked at the time of removal.

      (3) Where a vehicle is lawfully parked and is moved by emergency services for operational reasons—

      - (a) no liability attaches to the registered keeper for damage caused during removal; and
      - (b) any damage caused during removal is the liability of the relevant emergency service or authority.

      (4) For the purposes of this section, a vehicle is "illegally parked" if its presence on the relevant land or road at the relevant time constitutes a contravention of any enactment or road traffic regulation, or if it is present on private land without the permission of the owner or occupier.
  - num: '19'
    title: Appeals
    text: |
      (1) A registered keeper whose vehicle has been removed under section 14 may appeal to the local authority against the removal within 14 days of the date of the removal notice under section 16(2).

      (2) The grounds of appeal are—

      - (a) the vehicle was not illegally parked or obstructing access at the time of removal;
      - (b) the vehicle was taken and used without the registered keeper's consent, evidenced by a report made to the police prior to the removal, or the vehicle was recorded as stolen on the Police National Computer at the time of removal;
      - (c) removal was disproportionate in the circumstances;
      - (d) the procedure in section 15 was not followed.

      (3) The recovery of costs under section 16 is suspended pending the outcome of an appeal under this section.

      (4) The local authority must determine an appeal within 14 days of its receipt and must notify the appellant in writing of its decision and the reasons for it.

      (5) A person who is dissatisfied with the outcome of an appeal under this section may appeal to the relevant magistrates' court within 21 days of the local authority's decision.
  - num: '20'
    title: Disposal of unclaimed vehicles
    text: |
      (1) Where a vehicle removed under section 14 has not been reclaimed within 28 days of the removal notice under section 16(2), the local authority may dispose of the vehicle.

      (2) Before disposing of a vehicle under subsection (1), the local authority must—

      - (a) make reasonable enquiries to trace the registered keeper; and
      - (b) give notice of the intended disposal to the registered keeper where traceable.

      (3) The local authority may dispose of a vehicle by—

      - (a) sale at public auction; or
      - (b) destruction, where the vehicle has no significant resale value.

      (4) Any proceeds from sale under subsection (3)(a) must be applied—

      - (a) first, in satisfaction of all costs recoverable under section 16;
      - (b) secondly, any surplus must be paid to the registered keeper where their address is known, or held for 12 months and then paid into the local authority's general fund.
- title: General and Supplementary
  clauses:
  - num: '21'
    title: Extension of civil enforcement officer powers and constable powers
    text: |
      (1) Civil enforcement officers appointed by a local authority under the Traffic Management Act 2004 are authorised to exercise the powers conferred on local authorities by this Act, including—

      - (a) attending locations where vehicle removal has been requested under section 15;
      - (b) authorising removal of vehicles under section 14;
      - (c) issuing removal notices and cost recovery notices under section 16.

      - (a) carry their identity document issued by the local authority and produce it on request;
      - (b) comply with any code of practice issued under section 22;
      - (c) act jointly with a constable where there is a risk of confrontation or disorder.

      (3) A constable has the power to—

      - (a) attend locations where vehicle removal has been requested under section 15;
      - (b) authorise and oversee removal of vehicles under section 14;
      - (c) issue removal notices under section 16; and
      - (d) exercise any other power conferred on a local authority by Part 3 of this Act.

      (4) A constable exercising powers under subsection (3) must comply with any code of practice issued under section 22.

      (5) References in this Act to an authorised officer of the local authority are references to a civil enforcement officer acting under this section or a constable acting under subsection (3).
  - num: '22'

      (1) The Secretary of State must issue a code of practice governing—

      - (a) the exercise by constables of powers under Part 1;

  - num: '24'

- title: Interpretation and Final Provisions
  clauses:
  - num: '25'
    title: Definitions
    text: |
      In this Act—

      **"building"** includes any structure or part of a structure, whether permanent or temporary, that is capable of being occupied;

      **"constable"** means a member of a police force of any rank;

      **"driveway"** means a private road or access way providing vehicular access to residential or commercial premises;

      **"civil enforcement officer"** means a civil enforcement officer appointed by a local authority under the Traffic Management Act 2004, exercising powers under this Act in accordance with section 21;

      **"local authority"** means—

      - (a) in England, a district council, a London borough council, a metropolitan district council, a county council in an area without district councils, and the Common Council of the City of London;
      - (b) in Wales, a county or county borough council;
      - (c) in Scotland, a council constituted under section 2 of the Local Government etc. (Scotland) Act 1994;
      - (d) in Northern Ireland, a district council;

      **"occupier"** means the person who occupies land or premises as their home, place of business, or otherwise as a matter of right or permission;

      **"owner"** means the person entitled to possession of land or premises, whether as freeholder, leaseholder, or otherwise;

      **"personal land"** means land used primarily for private residential purposes, including gardens, private driveways, and curtilage of a dwelling;

      **"registered keeper"** means the person in whose name a vehicle is registered under the Vehicle Excise and Registration Act 1994;

      **"residential land"** means land forming part of or attached to a dwelling;

      **"trespass warning"** has the meaning given in section 2(1)(b);

      **"Trespass Warning Register"** has the meaning given in section 5;

      **"urban area"** and **"rural area"** have the meanings given in guidance issued under section 23;

      **"vehicle"** means any mechanically propelled vehicle intended or adapted for use on roads, including cars, vans, motorcycles, and caravans.

## Introduction

1. These Explanatory Notes relate to the Land Rights, Trespass and Vehicle Removal Bill. They have been prepared to assist the reader of the Bill and to help inform debate. They do not form part of the Bill and have not been endorsed by Parliament.

## Summary

{:start="2"}
2. The Bill gives police and local authorities new and strengthened powers to protect the rights of landowners and occupiers in three areas: trespass on personal and residential land; squatting; and vehicles parked illegally on private land or obstructing driveways.

## Policy background

{:start="3"}
3. The Bill addresses three related failures in the current law. First, trespass on personal land is a civil matter, leaving ordinary homeowners with no effective quick remedy. Second, the law on squatting does not adequately distinguish between occupied buildings (where owners need immediate protection) and unoccupied buildings (where a more nuanced approach is appropriate). Third, there is no effective power for councils to remove vehicles blocking private driveways, leaving residents without a practical remedy.

## Commentary on clauses

### Part 1 — Trespass on personal and residential land

{:start="4"}
4. Clause 1 gives constables an immediate power to direct a trespasser to leave residential or personal land. Failure to comply is a summary offence. This provides a fast, practical first-stage response.
5. Clause 2 requires a formal trespass warning to be issued and recorded whenever clause 1 is exercised. The warning is not a criminal record. It creates the paper trail for escalation.
6. Clause 3 provides for a fixed penalty notice — £150 for a second incident, £500 for a third — where a warning has previously been issued in respect of the same land. Payment discharges liability.
7. Clause 4 creates the offence of persistent trespass: three or more trespasses at the same location after a warning and at least one fixed penalty notice. The maximum sentence is 51 weeks' imprisonment. The defence provisions in clause 6 ensure the offence cannot be used to criminalise genuinely innocent conduct.
8. Clause 5 establishes the Trespass Warning Register as a national register maintained by the Secretary of State, accessible to all constables across England, Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland. This ensures a persistent trespasser cannot reset their warning history by crossing a force boundary. Records are not criminal records and expire after 3 years with no further activity. Individuals named on the register may access their own record at any time via GOV.UK One Login. Subsection (5) future-proofs integration with the Police National Computer or any successor database without mandating it immediately.

### Part 2 — Squatting

{:start="9"}
9. Clause 7 makes it a criminal offence to squat in an occupied building, giving police immediate removal power without requiring a court order. This extends and strengthens existing law.
10. Clause 8 establishes the right to remain in unoccupied buildings. Where a building has been continuously empty for 18 months and the owner cannot demonstrate genuine active intent to bring it back into use through at least one of four specified proofs, a qualifying occupant has the right to remain. The court confirms this right and records it. The 18-month initial period does not count towards the five-year ownership entitlement. The four proofs of intent — active planning permission, signed building contract, current marketing, or registration with the empty homes programme with a realistic return date — are substantive tests, not paper exercises.
11. Clause 9 governs reoccupation by the owner. An owner may apply for a reoccupation order where they genuinely intend to reoccupy within 60 days and can demonstrate the reoccupation is not designed to defeat the qualifying occupant's rights. Reoccupation must be maintained for a minimum of 12 months. Where an owner vacates within 24 months of the end of that minimum period, the qualifying occupant may apply for a court finding that the reoccupation was not genuine — if successful, the five-year clock is treated as having run continuously throughout, and an immediate ownership order may be made.
12. Clause 10 provides for ownership registration after five continuous years of confirmed occupation. The qualifying occupant applies to the Land Registry, which serves notice on the registered owner allowing 65 days to object. Grounds of objection are limited: disputed continuity of occupation, abandonment, or reasonable excuse for the building's prolonged emptiness. Reasonable excuse — serious illness, probate dispute, insolvency, or another circumstance that genuinely prevented the owner from bringing the building back into use — may only be raised at the registration stage, not to interrupt the right to remain during the five-year period. Agricultural land protections in subsection (10) ensure the provision cannot be used to sever buildings from working farms or to claim agricultural land.
13. Clause 11 requires welfare assessment and housing referral before removal where a qualifying occupant appears homeless. Removal may not be carried out until interim accommodation is offered, unless the offer is refused or significant prejudice would result from delay.
14. Clause 12 provides explicit and comprehensive protection for working agricultural land and farm buildings. Where any part of an agricultural holding is in productive use, the entire holding — including any unoccupied buildings — is protected from squatting rights regardless of how long individual buildings have been empty. Barns and outbuildings connected by ownership to working land cannot be claimed separately. Only where an entire holding, including all its land, has been wholly derelict and unused for the qualifying period do the right to remain and ownership registration apply — and then they apply to the whole holding, land and buildings together, under clause 10(12). This provides farmers with absolute certainty that working land and connected buildings cannot be subject to adverse occupation claims.

### Part 3 — Vehicle removal from private land

{:start="15"}
15. Clause 14 gives local authorities the power to remove vehicles from private land where parked without permission or obstructing a driveway.
16. Clauses 15 and 16 set out the request procedure and cost recovery mechanism. An authorised officer must attend within 4 hours of a request in an urban area and within 8 hours in a rural area; guidance under clause 23 defines which areas are urban and which are rural. All removal costs are recoverable from the registered keeper.
17. Clause 17 provides that where a vehicle is removed because it is illegally parked, the registered keeper bears liability for removal damage unless the authority was negligent.
18. Clause 18 addresses emergency services removal. Where a vehicle is illegally parked and moved by emergency services, the keeper is liable. Where a legally parked vehicle is moved for operational reasons, liability rests with the emergency service.
19. Clause 19 provides a clear appeals process — first to the local authority, then to the magistrates' court — with costs suspended pending the outcome.

## Financial implications

{:start="20"}
20. Fixed penalty notice revenue is retained by police forces and offsets administrative costs. Vehicle removal is cost-neutral: fees recover costs from registered keepers. Fast-track possession hearings require additional court resource, estimated at £5–10 million annually. This is offset by the reduction in full county court possession proceedings.

## Territorial extent

{:start="21"}
21. The Bill extends to England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland. Legislative consent is required from the Scottish Parliament and Northern Ireland Assembly for provisions affecting devolved competences. References to the Secretary of State are read as references to Scottish Ministers or Northern Ireland departments where functions are devolved.
