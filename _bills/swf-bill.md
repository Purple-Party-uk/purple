title: "Sovereign Wealth Fund Bill"
long_title: "A Bill to establish the United Kingdom Sovereign Wealth Fund as a permanent publicly owned investment fund; to provide for its governance, funding, and investment mandate; to establish six ring-fenced sub-accounts for pension, social care, green transition, public media, productive investment, and overseas diversification purposes; to protect the fund from dissolution or material alteration except by supermajority vote of the House of Commons; and for connected purposes."

parts:
  - title: "Establishment and Legal Status"
    clauses:
      - num: "1"
        title: "Establishment of the Sovereign Wealth Fund"
        text: |
          (1) There is established a body corporate known as "the United Kingdom Sovereign Wealth Fund" (in this Act referred to as "the Fund").

          (2) The Fund shall maintain its registered office in the United Kingdom.

          (3) The Fund is established for the permanent benefit of the people of the United Kingdom and is held in common ownership by the people of the United Kingdom.

          (4) The Fund is not the property of any government, political party, or private interest. It belongs to every citizen and permanent resident of the United Kingdom equally, in common, across all generations.

          (5) The Fund shall not be wound up, dissolved, or transferred to any other body except by an Act of Parliament passed in accordance with section 40.
      - num: "2"
        title: "Legal status and ownership"
        text: |
          (1) The Fund is a body corporate with perpetual succession.

          (2) The assets of the Fund are held on trust for the benefit of all present and future citizens and permanent residents of the United Kingdom.

          (3) No individual, company, or government has ownership rights over the assets of the Fund.

          (4) The assets of the Fund may not be—

          - (a) charged as security for any borrowing by His Majesty's Government;
          - (b) transferred to the Consolidated Fund or any other government account;
          - (c) used for any purpose not authorised by this Act.
      - num: "3"
        title: "Relationship with His Majesty's Government"
        text: |
          (1) The Fund is not a government department, non-departmental public body, or Crown body.

          (2) The Fund is not subject to direction by Ministers of the Crown except as expressly provided in this Act.

          (3) The Treasury is responsible for—

          - (a) collecting employer and personal contributions and paying them into the Fund in accordance with Part 2;
          - (b) laying the Fund's annual report before Parliament;
          - (c) introducing any legislation required to implement the Pension Reform Act in connection with the Fund.

          (4) The Fund shall report annually to Parliament through the Treasury but is not accountable to the Treasury for its investment decisions.
      - num: "4"
        title: "Prohibition on Ministerial direction"
        text: |
          (1) No Minister of the Crown may give directions to the Fund or the Board concerning—

          - (a) the selection of specific investments;
          - (b) the disposal of specific assets;
          - (c) the return targets of any sub-account;
          - (d) the geographic or sectoral allocation of investments within the parameters set by this Act;
          - (e) any other operational matter.

          (2) Any direction purporting to be given under this section is void and of no effect.

          (3) A Minister who knowingly attempts to direct the Fund or Board in contravention of subsection (1) commits a criminal offence and is liable—

          - (a) on summary conviction, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 12 months or a fine, or both;
          - (b) on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years or a fine, or both.
  - title: "Funding"
    clauses:
      - num: "5"
        title: "Employer contributions"
        text: |
          (1) Every employer must pay contributions to the Fund in accordance with Schedule 1.

          (2) The base contribution rate is 7.5% of payroll, allocated as follows—

          - (a) 6% to the Pension Sub-Account; and
          - (b) 1.5% to the Social Care Sub-Account.

          (3) Where an employer's CEO-to-median pay ratio exceeds 20:1, the contribution rate is increased in accordance with Schedule 1.

          (4) The maximum contribution rate under subsection (3) is 15% of payroll.

          (5) Contributions under this section are payable monthly in arrears and collected by His Majesty's Revenue and Customs.

          (6) Employer contributions are not counted as government revenue.
      - num: "6"
        title: "Personal contributions"
        text: |
          (1) Every individual with income above the National Income Threshold (as defined in section 46) must pay personal contributions to the Fund as follows—

          - (a) 1.5% of income above the National Income Threshold to the Pension Sub-Account; and
          - (b) 0.5% of income above the National Income Threshold to the Social Care Sub-Account.

          (2) Personal contributions are deducted at source through the PAYE system or collected through self-assessment returns, as appropriate.

          (3) Personal contributions are not counted as government revenue and do not reduce an individual's income for income tax purposes.

          (4) For individuals whose income falls below the National Income Threshold, the Secretary of State shall make contributions on their behalf at the rates specified in subsection (1), calculated against the National Income Threshold as the base. Full mechanics are set out in the Pension Reform Act.
      - num: "7"
        title: "Media levy"
        text: |
          (1) A levy of 0.5% is payable on all income received by every adult ordinarily resident in the United Kingdom, including National Income Threshold payments.

          (2) The levy applies to all income from whatever source, without exemption.

          (3) For recipients of National Income Threshold payments, the levy is deducted at source from the payment before it is made.

          (4) For individuals in employment or self-employment, the levy is collected through the PAYE system or self-assessment, as appropriate.

          (5) All revenue from the levy under this section is paid into the BBC and Media Sub-Account.

          (6) The levy under this section is permanent and does not expire upon the sunset of the corporate media surcharge under section 8.
      - num: "8"
        title: "Corporate media surcharge and sunset"
        text: |
          (1) A surcharge of 0.25% is payable on the UK revenue of every company in the high-margin corporate tier as defined in the relevant tax legislation.

          (2) All revenue from the surcharge under this section is paid into the BBC and Media Sub-Account.

          (3) The surcharge under this section terminates automatically ("the sunset") when—

          - (a) the BBC Stream overseas licensing revenue has exceeded £2 billion in each of two consecutive financial years; and
          - (b) an independent auditor appointed by the Board has certified that condition (a) is satisfied.

          (4) The sunset takes effect on the first day of the financial year following certification under subsection (3)(b).

          (5) Following the sunset, the surcharge may only be reinstated by an Act of Parliament passed by simple majority. It does not revive automatically for any reason.

          (6) If the condition in subsection (3)(a) has not been satisfied within 7 years of commencement of this Act, Parliament must vote on whether to extend, modify, or remove the surcharge. The surcharge cannot persist beyond 7 years without an affirmative vote of Parliament.
      - num: "9"
        title: "Environmental levy"
        text: |
          (1) Revenue from the following sources is paid into the Green Transition Sub-Account—

          - (a) the fossil fuel sectoral surcharge imposed under the relevant tax legislation;
          - (b) environmental producer levies imposed under the relevant environmental legislation.

          (2) The Treasury must pay such revenue into the Green Transition Sub-Account within 30 days of receipt.

          (3) For the avoidance of doubt, the environmental levy revenues are not counted as government revenue once paid into the Fund.
      - num: "10"
        title: "BBC and media commercial income"
        text: |
          (1) All commercial income of the BBC and its divisions flows into the BBC and Media Sub-Account.

          (2) "Commercial income" for the purposes of this section means—

          - (a) overseas content licensing revenue;
          - (b) platform licensing fees received from other broadcasters or streaming services;
          - (c) content sales to third parties;
          - (d) any other income generated by the BBC through commercial activity outside the United Kingdom.

          (3) Schedule 3 sets out qualifying receipts in further detail.

          (4) Commercial income paid into the BBC and Media Sub-Account under this section is reinvested within that sub-account in accordance with its endowment mandate under section 17.
      - num: "11"
        title: "Voluntary additional contributions"
        text: |
          (1) Any individual may make voluntary additional contributions to the Pension Sub-Account above the mandatory rate specified in section 6(1)(a).

          (2) Voluntary contributions under subsection (1) attract income tax relief at the contributor's marginal rate.

          (3) Employers may make voluntary enhanced contributions to the Pension Sub-Account on behalf of employees above the mandatory rate specified in section 5(2)(a).

          (4) Full mechanics of voluntary contributions are set out in the Pension Reform Act.
      - num: "12"
        title: "Treatment of contributions in public accounts"
        text: |
          (1) Employer and personal contributions to the Fund are not counted as government revenue in the national accounts.

          (2) Fund investment returns are not counted as government income.

          (3) Pension and social care payments made from the Fund are not counted as government expenditure.

          (4) The one-off BBC seed capital provided through the corporate media surcharge in year one appears in the government accounts as capital expenditure in accordance with normal accounting treatment.
  - title: "Sub-Account Architecture"
    clauses:
      - num: "13"
        title: "Establishment of sub-accounts"
        text: |
          (1) The Fund shall maintain six ring-fenced sub-accounts—

          - (a) the Pension Sub-Account;
          - (b) the Social Care Sub-Account;
          - (c) the Green Transition Sub-Account;
          - (d) the BBC and Media Sub-Account;
          - (e) the Productive Investment Sub-Account;
          - (f) the Overseas Diversification allocation.

          (2) Each sub-account is ring-fenced from the others and has its own—

          - (a) dedicated funding source;
          - (b) investment return mandate;
          - (c) permitted use of funds; and
          - (d) reporting obligations.

          (3) The Board must maintain separate accounts for each sub-account and report on each separately in the annual report.
      - num: "14"
        title: "Pension Sub-Account"
        text: |
          (1) The Pension Sub-Account is funded by—

          - (a) the 6% employer pension contributions under section 5(2)(a);
          - (b) the 1.5% personal pension contributions under section 6(1)(a);
          - (c) state contributions made on behalf of individuals below the National Income Threshold under section 6(4);
          - (d) voluntary additional contributions under section 11.

          (2) The investment return mandate of the Pension Sub-Account is to maximise long-term risk-adjusted returns within a prudent risk framework.

          (3) Individual pension entitlements are tracked within this sub-account in accordance with the Pension Reform Act.

          (4) The Fund shall pay defined minimum pensions to all qualifying citizens regardless of contribution record, with higher payments for those with longer contribution histories, in accordance with the Pension Reform Act.

          (5) Public sector pension schemes shall be migrated into this sub-account in accordance with the Pension Reform Act. All accrued defined benefit rights shall be honoured in full.

          (6) Pension payouts are treated as income for tax purposes in accordance with the Pension Reform Act.
      - num: "15"
        title: "Social Care Sub-Account"
        text: |
          (1) The Social Care Sub-Account is funded by—

          - (a) the 1.5% employer social care contributions under section 5(2)(b);
          - (b) the 0.5% personal social care contributions under section 6(1)(b).

          (2) The investment return mandate of the Social Care Sub-Account is identical to that of the Pension Sub-Account.

          (3) Funds in this sub-account may only be drawn for social care purposes as defined in subsection (4).

          (4) "Social care purposes" means—

          - (a) funding the provision of adult social care services in England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland;
          - (b) capital investment in social care infrastructure;
          - (c) such other purposes as Parliament may specify by resolution.

          (5) During the build phase, day-to-day social care costs continue to be funded from general taxation. Drawdown from this sub-account shall not commence until the Board certifies that investment returns are sufficient to cover annual social care costs without depleting the capital base.

          (6) Once the sub-account reaches self-sustaining status under subsection (5), the Board shall notify Parliament and the Secretary of State, who must within 90 days introduce regulations reducing the employer and personal contribution rates proportionately.
      - num: "16"
        title: "Green Transition Sub-Account"
        text: |
          (1) The Green Transition Sub-Account is funded by environmental levy revenues under section 9.

          (2) The investment return mandate of this sub-account is a blended mandate—

          - (a) energy assets are expected to generate commercial returns; and
          - (b) energy efficiency programmes are accepted as lower-return social investments.

          (3) The Board must report separately on commercial and social return elements of this sub-account in the annual report.

          (4) Funds in this sub-account may only be invested in green infrastructure and green transition activities, meaning any infrastructure, technology, programme, or asset whose primary purpose is to reduce carbon emissions, transition away from fossil fuels, improve energy efficiency, or restore and protect natural ecosystems — provided that the investment is not extractive in nature and does not primarily generate returns through the ownership of scarce resources rather than productive activity.

          (5) The Board must publish guidance on the meaning of "extractive in nature" for the purposes of subsection (4) and review that guidance at least every three years.

          (6) For the avoidance of doubt, permitted investments under subsection (4) include but are not limited to renewable energy generation, grid infrastructure, energy storage, heat networks, building efficiency programmes, green hydrogen, and nature restoration. This list is illustrative only and does not limit the scope of subsection (4).

          (7) No funds from the Pension or Social Care Sub-Accounts may be redirected to this sub-account for any reason.
      - num: "17"
        title: "BBC and Media Sub-Account"
        text: |
          (1) The BBC and Media Sub-Account is funded by—

          - (a) the 0.5% universal media levy under section 7;
          - (b) the 0.25% corporate media surcharge under section 8 (until sunset);
          - (c) all BBC commercial income under section 10.

          (2) The long-term investment mandate of this sub-account is endowment building — the accumulation of sufficient capital that investment returns cover the BBC's annual budget in full without reliance on the media levy.

          (3) Until the endowment threshold is reached, the BBC's annual budget is funded from the combination of—

          - (a) the media levy;
          - (b) investment returns on accumulated capital; and
          - (c) commercial income.

          (4) The Board shall certify to Parliament when investment returns from this sub-account are sufficient to cover the BBC's annual budget. Upon such certification, the Secretary of State must within 90 days introduce regulations reducing the media levy to the minimum rate required to maintain the endowment.

          (5) Funds in this sub-account may only be used for—

          - (a) the annual budget of the BBC and its divisions as approved by Parliament;
          - (b) capital investment in BBC infrastructure and technology;
          - (c) accumulation within the endowment.

          (6) Full BBC funding mechanics are set out in the BBC Reform and Public Broadcasting Act.
      - num: "18"
        title: "Productive Investment Sub-Account"
        text: |
          (1) The Productive Investment Sub-Account is funded by a proportion of general investment returns from the wider Fund, set annually by the Board within the following parameters—

          - (a) not less than 5% of total annual Fund returns; and
          - (b) not more than 20% of total annual Fund returns.

          (2) The investment mandate of this sub-account is direct investment in the United Kingdom productive economy.

          (3) Permitted investments are—

          - (a) minority equity stakes in worker cooperatives in accordance with section 26;
          - (b) long-term patient capital loans and equity to small and medium enterprises;
          - (c) co-investment with universities and research institutions in research and development;
          - (d) early-stage funding for cooperative startups and high-potential productive businesses.

          (4) The prohibited investments in section 24 apply to this sub-account.

          (5) At least 60% of the Productive Investment Sub-Account must be deployed outside London and the South East of England in each financial year.

          (6) The Board must report annually on compliance with subsection (5), with a breakdown of investment by region and nation.

          (7) The regional minimum in subsection (5) may only be varied by an Act of Parliament.
      - num: "19"
        title: "Overseas Diversification"
        text: |
          (1) Up to 25% of the total value of the Fund may be held in overseas assets.

          (2) The 25% ceiling in subsection (1) is a maximum, not a target. The Fund begins domestic-heavy and moves toward the ceiling as it matures.

          (3) Permitted overseas instruments are—

          - (a) sovereign bonds issued by stable democracies; and
          - (b) a diversified international equity portfolio managed directly by the Fund through its own internally administered passive index, constructed and maintained by the Chief Investment Officer in accordance with the ethical exclusions in subsection (4). The Fund shall not invest in third-party index funds or pay external managers to administer the overseas equity portfolio.

          (4) The following are excluded from the overseas equity portfolio—

          - (a) fossil fuel extraction and production companies;
          - (b) arms and weapons manufacturers, with the exception of companies whose primary purpose is the manufacture of defence equipment for the armed forces of the United Kingdom or its treaty allies;
          - (c) tobacco production companies;
          - (d) companies primarily engaged in gambling operations;
          - (e) companies domiciled or operating primarily in authoritarian states as designated by the Secretary of State by order;
          - (f) companies whose primary business model is extractive in nature, meaning the generation of returns through ownership of scarce assets rather than productive activity;
          - (g) any company that would be a prohibited investment under section 24 if it were a domestic investee;
          - (h) companies primarily registered in a designated tax haven jurisdiction as defined in the relevant tax legislation where that company does not have genuine economic substance in that jurisdiction — meaning real employees, real offices, and real business activity proportionate to the assets or income booked there. A company whose registration in a designated jurisdiction reflects legitimate operational presence rather than tax avoidance is not excluded under this paragraph.

          (5) For the purposes of subsection (4)(h), the Board must apply the genuine economic substance test used in the tax haven withholding tax legislation, applied with necessary modifications to the context of overseas equity investment. The Board must publish its methodology for applying this test annually.

          (6) The Board must publish the methodology and constituent list of the overseas equity portfolio annually.

          (7) No single country may represent more than 10% of the overseas allocation.
      - num: "20"
        title: "Prohibition on transfers between sub-accounts"
        text: |
          (1) No funds may be transferred between sub-accounts except by an Act of Parliament.

          (2) Any purported transfer between sub-accounts made otherwise than in accordance with subsection (1) is void and of no effect.

          (3) The Board must include in its annual report a statement confirming that no transfers between sub-accounts have been made during the year.
      - num: "21"
        title: "Domestic and overseas investment ratio"
        text: |
          (1) At least 75% of the total value of the Fund must be held in United Kingdom assets at all times.

          (2) The domestic minimum in subsection (1) and the overseas ceiling in section 19(1) may only be varied by an Act of Parliament passed in accordance with section 41.

          (3) All sub-accounts count toward the domestic 75% where their assets are UK-based.
  - title: "Investment Mandate"
    clauses:
      - num: "22"
        title: "General investment principles"
        text: |
          (1) In making investment decisions the Board must have regard to—

          - (a) maximising long-term risk-adjusted returns for pension and social care sub-accounts;
          - (b) the specific mandates of each sub-account;
          - (c) the long-term productive capacity of the United Kingdom economy;
          - (d) environmental sustainability;
          - (e) the geographic rebalancing of investment away from London and the South East.

          (2) The Board must not have regard to short-term political considerations in making investment decisions.
      - num: "23"
        title: "Permitted investments"
        text: |
          (1) Subject to the specific mandates in Part 3, the Fund may invest in—

          - (a) United Kingdom productive businesses;
          - (b) worker cooperatives in accordance with section 26;
          - (c) United Kingdom infrastructure;
          - (d) research and development;
          - (e) overseas assets within the parameters of section 19.
      - num: "24"
        title: "Prohibited investments"
        text: |
          (1) The Fund may not invest in—

          - (a) financial services speculation;
          - (b) residential property held for rental extraction;
          - (c) share buyback programmes;
          - (d) private equity vehicles whose primary purpose is asset extraction;
          - (e) fossil fuel extraction assets (save where held as part of a broad passive index within the overseas allocation);
          - (f) any activity that primarily extracts rather than creates value as determined by the Board in accordance with guidance issued under subsection (2).

          (2) The Board must publish guidance on the meaning of "primarily extracts rather than creates value" for the purposes of subsection (1)(f) and review that guidance at least every three years.

          (3) The prohibitions in this section are written into the Fund's statutory investment mandate and cannot be waived by the Board or by Ministerial direction.
      - num: "25"
        title: "Regional investment requirement"
        text: |
          (1) At least 60% of the Productive Investment Sub-Account must be deployed outside London and the South East of England in each financial year.

          (2) "London and the South East of England" means the regions so designated by the Office for National Statistics.

          (3) Compliance with subsection (1) is reported annually to Parliament.

          (4) The Board may apply to Parliament by resolution to vary the percentage in subsection (1) where it certifies that compliance would materially harm the sub-account's investment returns. Parliament may approve a variation for a period not exceeding three financial years.
      - num: "26"
        title: "Cooperative equity investment"
        text: |
          (1) The Fund may hold minority equity stakes in worker cooperatives subject to the following conditions—

          - (a) the Fund's stake must not exceed 15% of the total equity of the cooperative;
          - (b) no cooperative may issue more than 15% of its equity to the Fund or any other external body in aggregate;
          - (c) the Fund holds non-voting economic rights only — no governance or voting rights attach to its stake;
          - (d) workers retain full democratic control of the cooperative at all times.

          (2) The 15% ceiling in subsection (1)(a) and (b) is absolute and permanent. It may not be waived by the Board, by agreement with the cooperative, or by Ministerial direction.

          (3) Any equity arrangement purporting to grant the Fund voting rights in a cooperative is void.

          (4) The Fund may additionally provide patient capital loans to cooperatives. Such loans do not count toward the 15% equity ceiling.
      - num: "27"
        title: "Private pension schemes"
        text: |
          (1) Private pension schemes may continue to operate and compete with the Fund.

          (2) Private pension schemes carry no state guarantee. They are investments that may increase or decrease in value.

          (3) Notwithstanding subsection (2), where criminal conduct by a private pension scheme operator causes loss to scheme members—

          - (a) the scheme operator is personally liable for that loss;
          - (b) assets of the operator are subject to clawback to make good the loss;
          - (c) criminal liability attaches to directors and officers who were knowingly involved.

          (4) Full mechanics of private pension scheme regulation and competition are set out in the Pension Reform Act.
  - title: "Governance"
    clauses:
      - num: "28"
        title: "The SWF Board"
        text: |
          (1) The Fund is governed by a Board consisting of—

          - (a) a Chair;
          - (b) between 6 and 10 other members;
          - (c) a Chief Investment Officer appointed under section 32; and
          - (d) an Accounting Officer designated under section 33.

          (2) The majority of Board members must have demonstrable expertise in at least one of—

          - (a) long-term investment management;
          - (b) macroeconomics;
          - (c) cooperative and social enterprise finance;
          - (d) environmental finance and transition economics;
          - (e) pension and social care funding.

          (3) No Board member may simultaneously hold—

          - (a) ministerial office;
          - (b) a seat in either House of Parliament;
          - (c) a senior position in a financial institution that is an investee of the Fund;
          - (d) any office the holding of which would create a material conflict of interest.
      - num: "29"
        title: "Board appointments"
        text: |
          (1) Board members are appointed through the Independent Nominations Panel established under the Public Appointments and Democratic Participation Act.

          (2) The Independent Nominations Panel produces a shortlist of candidates based on merit, relevant expertise, and independence from political and commercial interests.

          (3) Final appointment decisions are made by a citizens' jury of between 15 and 20 citizens drawn by stratified random selection from the electoral register.

          (4) The citizens' jury's decision is binding. No Minister has any role in shortlisting or appointment.

          (5) Parliament confirms appointments by simple majority vote within 28 days of the citizens' jury's decision. Parliament may only reject an appointment on grounds of a material conflict of interest not identified by the Panel.

          (6) Schedule 4 sets out the appointment procedure in further detail.

          (7) Board members serve fixed terms of five years and may serve a maximum of two terms.
      - num: "30"
        title: "Board duties and powers"
        text: |
          (1) The Board must—

          - (a) act in accordance with the investment mandate in Part 4;
          - (b) maintain the sub-account architecture in Part 3;
          - (c) publish annual reports and accounts under section 34;
          - (d) publish investment decisions under section 35;
          - (e) appear before the Parliamentary Select Committee under section 36;
          - (f) commission independent audits under section 37.

          (2) The Board may—

          - (a) appoint investment managers to manage specific sub-accounts or asset classes;
          - (b) enter into co-investment arrangements with other sovereign wealth funds, pension funds, or public bodies;
          - (c) establish subsidiary vehicles for specific investment purposes.

          (3) The Board may not—

          - (a) transfer assets between sub-accounts;
          - (b) invest in prohibited categories under section 24;
          - (c) accept direction from Ministers under section 4;
          - (d) take any action that would require a supermajority under section 40 without that supermajority having been obtained.
      - num: "31"
        title: "Conflicts of interest"
        text: |
          (1) A Board member who has a material interest in a matter before the Board must—

          - (a) declare that interest at the beginning of the relevant meeting;
          - (b) withdraw from the meeting for the duration of deliberation and voting on that matter.

          (2) The Board must maintain a public register of declared interests updated quarterly.

          (3) Undisclosed conflicts of interest constitute a criminal offence. A Board member convicted under this subsection is liable on conviction on indictment to imprisonment not exceeding 2 years or a fine, or both.
      - num: "32"
        title: "Chief Investment Officer"
        text: |
          (1) The Board shall appoint a Chief Investment Officer responsible for the day-to-day management of the Fund's investments.

          (2) The Chief Investment Officer is appointed by the Board following an open competitive process.

          (3) The Chief Investment Officer reports to the Board and has no reporting relationship to any Minister.
      - num: "33"
        title: "Accounting Officer"
        text: |
          (1) The Board shall designate an Accounting Officer in accordance with section 5 of the Government Resources and Accounts Act 2000, applied with necessary modifications.

          (2) The Accounting Officer is personally responsible for the regularity and propriety of the Fund's finances.
      - num: "34"
        title: "Remuneration of Board members and staff"
        text: |
          (1) All remuneration paid by the Fund — to Board members, the Chief Investment Officer, the Accounting Officer, and all other employees and contractors — must be set in accordance with the Civil Service pay spine.

          (2) The hard ceiling on any individual's remuneration from the Fund in any financial year is £350,000. This ceiling—

          - (a) applies universally regardless of role, seniority, or commercial market rates for equivalent positions;
          - (b) cannot be waived by the Board or by any other body;
          - (c) may only be varied by an Act of Parliament.

          (3) Any remuneration above £200,000 requires explicit Board approval. The Board must—

          - (a) publish the individual's name and role;
          - (b) provide a written justification explaining why the role and the individual's contribution warrants pay at that level;
          - (c) confirm the remuneration does not exceed the £350,000 ceiling;
          - (d) publish the justification within 30 days of the contract being agreed.

          (4) The Fund must publish complete pay data annually for every individual receiving remuneration from the Fund above the National Income Threshold, including name, role, and total annual remuneration.

          (5) The rationale for the pay framework is that the Fund is held in common ownership by the people of the United Kingdom. Its staff are stewards of national wealth. Pay structures must reflect public accountability rather than private financial sector norms.
  - title: "Transparency and Accountability"
    clauses:
      - num: "35"
        title: "Annual report and accounts"
        text: |
          (1) The Board must publish an annual report and accounts within four months of the end of each financial year.

          (2) The annual report must include—

          - (a) a full account of all investments held in each sub-account;
          - (b) the investment return of each sub-account;
          - (c) compliance with the regional investment requirement under section 25;
          - (d) compliance with the domestic and overseas investment ratio under section 21;
          - (e) a statement confirming no transfers between sub-accounts;
          - (f) the remuneration of all Board members and senior staff;
          - (g) progress toward the social care self-sustaining threshold;
          - (h) progress toward the BBC and Media endowment threshold;
          - (i) the BBC corporate surcharge status and, where applicable, sunset certification.

          (3) The annual report and accounts must be laid before Parliament by the Treasury within 14 days of publication.
      - num: "36"
        title: "Publication of investment decisions"
        text: |
          (1) The Board must publish details of all investment decisions above £50 million within 30 days of the decision being made.

          (2) Publication under subsection (1) must include—

## Introduction

1. These Explanatory Notes relate to the Sovereign Wealth Fund Bill. They have been prepared to assist the reader of the Bill and to help inform debate. They do not form part of the Bill and have not been endorsed by Parliament.

## Summary

{:start="2"}
2. The Bill establishes the United Kingdom Sovereign Wealth Fund as a permanently publicly owned investment fund. It creates six ring-fenced sub-accounts for pension, social care, green transition, public media, productive investment, and overseas diversification. It sets out the Fund's governance, funding streams, and investment mandate. It protects the Fund from abolition or material alteration except by a two-thirds majority of the House of Commons.

## Policy background

{:start="3"}
3. Britain is one of the few advanced economies without a sovereign wealth fund. The Bill implements a policy of building permanent national wealth through productive long-term investment, replacing the current system of pay-as-you-go public obligations with a funded endowment model.

## Commentary on clauses

### Part 1 — Establishment and legal status

{:start="4"}
4. Clause 1 establishes the Fund as a permanent body corporate. The prohibition on winding up without a supermajority Act is fundamental to the Fund's constitutional protection.
5. Clause 2 establishes that Fund assets are held on trust for present and future citizens. This trust status prevents assets being treated as government property available for general expenditure.
6. Clause 4 makes it a criminal offence for a Minister to direct the Fund's investment decisions. This is the hardest possible protection against political interference and reflects the principle that operational investment decisions must be entirely insulated from government.

### Part 2 — Funding

{:start="7"}
7. Clause 5 replaces employer National Insurance contributions with employer SWF contributions at a base rate of 10%, with a wage ratio surcharge reaching 15% for employers with CEO-to-median pay ratios above 75:1. This rewards fair pay structures whilst penalising extraction.
8. Clause 7 establishes the universal 0.5% media levy on all income including NIT payments. Its universality — applying to every adult regardless of income source — is deliberate. It creates a genuine sense of collective ownership of the public media platform.
9. Clause 8 provides for the corporate media surcharge with a hard statutory sunset. The sunset is automatic upon independent certification that the threshold is met — Parliament cannot extend it by inaction.

### Part 3 — Sub-account architecture

{:start="10"}
10. Clause 20 makes the prohibition on transfers between sub-accounts absolute. No transfer can occur without primary legislation passed by supermajority. This prevents creative accounting that would undermine the integrity of each sub-account's dedicated purpose.
11. Clause 18(5) imposes the 60% regional investment minimum on the Productive Investment Sub-Account only. The other sub-accounts are deliberately excluded from geographic mandates — imposing regional targets on pension or social care assets would create fiduciary duty conflicts and potential legal challenge from beneficiaries.

### Part 4 — Investment mandate

{:start="12"}
12. Clause 26 sets out the cooperative equity mechanism. The 15% ceiling on external equity in any cooperative is absolute and unamendable by the Board or Ministers. This permanently prevents the gradual financialisation of the cooperative sector through SWF investment — the SWF provides capital without ever acquiring control.
13. Clause 27 removes the state guarantee from private pension schemes prospectively. Members of existing schemes retain their existing guarantees. New members of existing schemes from commencement, and all members of new schemes established after commencement, have no state guarantee. This creates a genuine level competitive playing field — citizens choosing a private scheme do so knowingly, with full disclosure that their investment carries risk.

### Part 7 — Constitutional protection

{:start="14"}
14. Clause 39 is the most constitutionally significant provision in the Bill. The two-thirds supermajority requirement for abolition or material alteration, combined with the self-entrenching provision in subsection (4), makes the Fund as constitutionally durable as any unentrenched provision in British law can be. A future government with a large majority but less than two-thirds cannot dismantle the Fund unilaterally.
15. Subsection (4) closes the obvious loophole — a government first reducing the threshold by simple majority, then abolishing the Fund by simple majority. Both steps require two-thirds.

## Financial implications

{:start="16"}
16. The Fund does not appear as a government programme in the national accounts. Contributions are not taxes; returns are not government income; pension and social care payments are not government expenditure. The Fund is a separate legal entity managing national assets.
17. Annual inflows from employer and personal contributions are estimated at £86–105 billion. The Fund reaches significant scale within three years and self-sustaining social care coverage within seven to eight years.
18. The one-off BBC seed capital of £8 billion appears as capital expenditure in year one. It is funded by the ring-fenced corporate surcharge and represents a finite, bounded commitment.
