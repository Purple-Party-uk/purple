title: "Crown Reform Bill"
long_title: "A Bill to make provision for the transfer of Crown property to public ownership; to abolish the Sovereign Grant and provide for the remuneration of the Monarch and Heir Apparent; to remove legal exemptions applying to the Crown and members of the Royal Family; to abolish Royal Assent, King's Consent, and the Privy Council; to require publication of communications between the Monarch and Ministers; to provide for parliamentary votes on the continuation of the monarchy upon succession; and for connected purposes."

parts:
  - title: "Property and Crown Estate"
    clauses:
      - title: "Transfer of Crown property"
        text: |
          (1) On the commencement of this Act, the following property vests in the Crown Estate—

          - (a) all property, rights, and interests of the Duchy of Lancaster;
          - (b) all property, rights, and interests of the Duchy of Cornwall;
          - (c) the Royal Collection and all items comprised within it;
          - (d) all other property held by or on behalf of the Crown not otherwise specified in Schedule 1.

          (2) The property specified in Schedule 1 is transferred to the Crown Estate by operation of this subsection.

          (3) All contracts, leases, licences, and other legal obligations relating to property transferred under subsections (1) and (2) continue in force with the Crown Estate as successor in title.

          (4) The transfer under this section operates without requiring any surrender, conveyance, or other instrument of transfer.

          (5) His Majesty's Land Registry must, upon application by the Crown Estate, register the Crown Estate as proprietor of any registered property transferred under this section without requiring production of any deed or other evidence of title.
      - title: "Crown Estate management"
        text: |
          (1) The Crown Estate shall be managed by the Crown Estate Commissioners as part of His Majesty's Treasury.

          (2) All revenue generated by the Crown Estate shall be paid into the Consolidated Fund.

          (3) The Crown Estate may continue to use the brands "Duchy of Lancaster" and "Duchy of Cornwall" for commercial purposes.

          (4) The Treasury may make payments from the Consolidated Fund to the Crown Estate for the maintenance and upkeep of Crown Estate property.

          (5) The Crown Estate shall maintain all property transferred under section 1 to appropriate standards, having regard to historic and architectural significance where applicable.
      - title: "Residence rights of the Monarch"
        text: |
          (1) The Monarch is entitled to reside at Buckingham Palace without charge.

          (2) The entitlement under subsection (1) includes—

          - (a) reasonable private accommodation for the Monarch and their spouse or long-term partner;
          - (b) working office space for carrying out official duties.

          (3) The Monarch is not entitled to reside at any other Crown Estate property without payment in accordance with section 4.

          (4) The entitlement under subsection (1) is personal to the Monarch and does not extend to—

          - (a) the Heir Apparent;
          - (b) any other member of the Royal Family;
          - (c) any other person except as provided in subsection (2)(a).
      - title: "Payment for use of Crown Estate property"
        text: |
          (1) Any use of Crown Estate property by the Monarch or any member of the Royal Family for purposes other than official duties is subject to payment at market rate.

          (2) Market rate shall be determined by independent valuation obtained by the Crown Estate from a qualified valuer.

          (3) "Official duties" has the meaning given in section 25.

          (4) The Crown Estate must publish annually—

          - (a) details of any property used by the Monarch or Royal Family under this section;
          - (b) the market rate charged;
          - (c) payments received.
      - title: "Private property"
        text: |
          (1) Nothing in this Part affects the private ownership of—

          - (a) Balmoral Castle and its estate;
          - (b) Sandringham House and its estate;
          - (c) any other property personally owned by the Monarch or members of the Royal Family that was not held as Crown property immediately before commencement of this Act.

          (2) Property described in subsection (1) is subject to all taxation and legal obligations that apply to privately owned property, without exemption.
  - title: "Taxation and Remuneration"
    clauses:
      - title: "Abolition of tax exemptions"
        text: |
          (1) From the commencement of this Act, the Monarch and all members of the Royal Family are liable to taxation without exemption as follows—

          - (a) income tax on all income from whatever source;
          - (b) capital gains tax on disposals of assets;
          - (c) inheritance tax on estates;
          - (d) national insurance contributions;
          - (e) value added tax on purchases of goods and services;
          - (f) council tax on properties;
          - (g) any other tax, duty, or levy imposed by law.

          (2) No exemption, relief, or special treatment applies to the Monarch or any member of the Royal Family by virtue of their status.

          (3) This section applies—

          - (a) to income arising, gains accruing, and transactions occurring on or after the commencement of this Act;
          - (b) without retrospective effect to income, gains, or transactions before commencement.

          (4) For the purposes of this section, "member of the Royal Family" includes any person who is or was entitled to the style of Royal Highness or who is in the line of succession to the Crown.
      - title: "Abolition of Sovereign Grant"
        text: |
          (1) The Sovereign Grant Act 2011 is repealed.

          (2) No further payments shall be made under the Sovereign Grant from the commencement of this Act.

          (3) Any Sovereign Grant payment made for the financial year in which this Act commences, to the extent not yet spent, shall be transferred to the Royal Household established under section 9.
      - title: "Salaries"
        text: |
          (1) The Monarch shall be paid a salary of £750,000 per annum.

          (2) The Heir Apparent shall be paid a salary of £150,000 per annum.

          (3) Salaries under subsections (1) and (2) shall be—

          - (a) paid monthly in arrears from the Consolidated Fund;
          - (b) increased on 1st April each year in line with the percentage increase in the Consumer Prices Index for the 12 months ending with the preceding December;
          - (c) subject to income tax and national insurance contributions.

          (4) No other member of the Royal Family is entitled to payment from public funds by virtue of their status, except as provided in section 10.

          (5) The salaries specified in subsections (1) and (2) may only be varied by Act of Parliament.
      - title: "Royal Household as executive non-departmental public body"
        text: |
          (1) There shall be a body corporate known as "the Royal Household" which shall be an executive non-departmental public body.

          (2) The Royal Household shall be sponsored by the Deputy Prime Minister.

          (3) Schedule 1 to the Public Bodies Act 2011 is amended to include the Royal Household in the list of public bodies subject to that Act.

          (4) The functions of the Royal Household are—

          - (a) to organise and facilitate the official duties of the Monarch;
          - (b) to employ staff necessary for the performance of those duties;
          - (c) to manage expenditure on official duties in accordance with its budget;
          - (d) to maintain working spaces within Buckingham Palace;
          - (e) to publish information in accordance with this Act.

          (5) The Royal Household shall be funded by—

          - (a) an annual budget approved by Parliament through Supply estimates;
          - (b) any other sums voted by Parliament for its purposes.

          (6) The Royal Household is subject to—

          - (a) audit by the Comptroller and Auditor General;
          - (b) examination by the National Audit Office;
          - (c) scrutiny by the Public Accounts Committee;
          - (d) the Freedom of Information Act 2000;
          - (e) the Public Contracts Regulations 2015;
          - (f) all other legislation applicable to public bodies.

          (7) The Royal Household must publish annual reports and accounts in accordance with Managing Public Money and Treasury guidance.

          (8) The board of the Royal Household shall consist of—

          - (a) a Chair appointed by the Deputy Prime Minister following the procedure in the Public Appointments Order in Council 2019;
          - (b) between 4 and 8 other members similarly appointed;
          - (c) an accounting officer designated in accordance with section 5 of the Government Resources and Accounts Act 2000.

          (9) Staff of the Royal Household shall be appointed in accordance with Civil Service recruitment procedures and shall be subject to the Civil Service Code.
      - title: "Payment of other members of Royal Family"
        text: |
          (1) The Royal Household may employ members of the Royal Family to perform official duties only where—

          - (a) it is in the public interest to do so; and
          - (b) the employment has been approved by the board of the Royal Household.

          (2) In determining whether employment is in the public interest under subsection (1)(a), the Royal Household must have regard to—

          - (a) whether the duties could reasonably be performed by a non-royal employee;
          - (b) whether the member's royal status provides a unique benefit in performing the duties;
          - (c) the cost-effectiveness of the arrangement;
          - (d) the principle of merit-based appointment.

          (3) The Royal Household must not employ a member of the Royal Family where—

          - (a) the primary purpose is to provide income to that member;
          - (b) a non-royal candidate of equal or greater merit is available;
          - (c) the employment would create a perception of nepotism or preferential treatment.

          (4) Any member employed under this section shall be paid—

          - (a) in accordance with Civil Service pay scales appropriate to the duties performed;
          - (b) expenses necessarily incurred in the performance of official duties in accordance with Civil Service expenses policies.

          (5) Payment under this section may only be made for official duties as defined in section 25.

          (6) No payment may be made under this section for—

          - (a) charitable work undertaken by a member of the Royal Family;
          - (b) private engagements;
          - (c) activities not organised by or on behalf of His Majesty's Government.

          (7) The Royal Household must publish quarterly—

          - (a) the names of any members of the Royal Family employed under this section;
          - (b) the duties they perform;
          - (c) their salary range;
          - (d) expenses claimed and paid;
          - (e) a statement explaining how the employment meets the public interest test under subsection (1)(a).

          (8) The board of the Royal Household must review all employment of Royal Family members annually and may terminate any employment that no longer meets the public interest test.
  - title: "Legislative Scrutiny and Legal Exemptions"
    clauses:
      - title: "Abolition of Royal Assent"
        text: |
          (1) The requirement for Royal Assent for Bills to become Acts of Parliament is abolished.

          (2) A Bill becomes an Act of Parliament when—

          - (a) it has been passed by the House of Commons;
          - (b) it has been passed by the House of Lords or the requirements of the Parliament Acts 1911 and 1949 have been satisfied; and
          - (c) the Speaker of the House of Commons and the Lord Speaker have each certified in writing that the requirements of paragraph (a) or (b), as applicable, have been satisfied.

          (3) Certification under subsection (2)(c) must be—

          - (a) endorsed on the Bill;
          - (b) signed by both the Speaker and the Lord Speaker;
          - (c) dated with the date on which the Bill becomes an Act.

          (4) An Act certified in accordance with subsection (3) shall be enrolled and published in accordance with existing procedures, modified as necessary to reflect the abolition of Royal Assent.

          (5) All references in any enactment to Royal Assent shall be read as references to certification under this section.

          (6) This section comes into force immediately upon this Act receiving Royal Assent, which shall be the final occasion on which Royal Assent is given.
      - title: "Abolition of King's Consent"
        text: |
          (1) The requirement to obtain King's Consent (or Queen's Consent) before Parliament may debate a Bill is abolished.

          (2) No Bill or motion before Parliament requires the consent of the Monarch before it may be—

          - (a) introduced;
          - (b) debated;
          - (c) amended;
          - (d) voted upon.

          (3) The Standing Orders of both Houses of Parliament shall be amended as necessary to remove all references to King's Consent or Queen's Consent.
      - title: "Abolition of Privy Council"
        text: |
          (1) The Privy Council is abolished.

          (2) All functions previously performed by or with the advice of the Privy Council shall be performed by the relevant Secretary of State or other Minister of the Crown.

          (3) Schedule 3 makes provision for the approval of Orders in Council and related matters.

          (4) All references in any enactment to—

          - (a) the Privy Council;
          - (b) the Lords of Her Majesty's Most Honourable Privy Council;
          - (c) Orders in Council requiring the advice of the Privy Council;

          shall be read in accordance with Schedule 3.

          (5) The office of Lord President of the Council is abolished.

          (6) Appointments previously requiring Privy Council approval shall be made by the relevant Secretary of State.

          (7) Royal Charters shall be granted by the relevant Secretary of State (usually the Secretary of State for Business and Trade) following such consultation as appears appropriate.
      - title: "Access to legislation"
        text: |
          (1) The Monarch has the same rights as any other person to—

          - (a) view published Bills and draft legislation;
          - (b) respond to public consultations;
          - (c) make representations through the ordinary consultation process.

          (2) The Monarch has no right to—

          - (a) view draft Bills before publication;
          - (b) request changes to legislation outside the public consultation process;
          - (c) receive advance notice of legislation before it is made available to the public.

          (3) If the Monarch makes representations through the public consultation process, these shall be published in the same manner as representations from other consultees.
      - title: "Publication of communications with Ministers"
        text: |
          (1) The Royal Household must publish all written communications between the Monarch and any Minister of the Crown that relate to—

          - (a) proposed or existing legislation;
          - (b) government policy;
          - (c) official duties of the Monarch.

          (2) The Royal Household must publish minutes or summaries of all meetings between the Monarch and Ministers (other than the weekly audience with the Prime Minister) that relate to matters specified in subsection (1).

          (3) Publication under this section must occur—

          - (a) as soon as reasonably practicable; and
          - (b) in any event within 14 days of the communication or meeting.

          (4) Publications under this section must be—

          - (a) published on the Royal Household's website;
          - (b) freely accessible to the public;
          - (c) retained and archived in accordance with public records legislation.

          (5) Each publication under subsection (1) must include a statement that—

          "The views expressed by His Majesty the King carry no legal weight and do not bind His Majesty's Government or Parliament in any respect."

          (6) The weekly audience between the Monarch and the Prime Minister remains private and is not subject to publication under this section.

          (7) Nothing in this section requires publication of information that would prejudice—

          - (a) national security;
          - (b) the prevention or detection of crime;
          - (c) the personal safety of any individual;

          provided that the Royal Household publishes a redacted version indicating that material has been withheld and the grounds for doing so.
      - title: "Removal of statutory exemptions"
        text: |
          (1) All exemptions, immunities, and special treatment applying to—

          - (a) the Crown;
          - (b) the Monarch;
          - (c) members of the Royal Family;
          - (d) the Royal Household;
          - (e) Crown property or interests;

          by virtue of their status, are abolished.

          (2) Without limiting the generality of subsection (1), the exemptions removed include those relating to—

          - (a) employment law;
          - (b) the Freedom of Information Act 2000;
          - (c) the Data Protection Act 2018;
          - (d) health and safety legislation;
          - (e) planning legislation;
          - (f) the Equality Act 2010;
          - (g) publication of wills under probate rules;
          - (h) any other legislation listed in Schedule 2.

          (3) The Monarch, members of the Royal Family, and the Royal Household are subject to all legislation as if they were private individuals or bodies, as applicable.

          (4) For the avoidance of doubt, this section applies to both civil and criminal law.

          (5) The appropriate Minister may by regulations make such amendments to existing legislation as appear necessary or expedient in consequence of this section.

          (6) Regulations under subsection (5) are subject to the affirmative procedure.
  - title: "Succession and Parliamentary Abolition"
    clauses:
      - title: "Parliamentary vote on continuation of monarchy"
        text: |
          (1) Within 28 days of the death of the Monarch, each House of Parliament must vote on the following question—

          "That the monarchy should continue."

          (2) The votes shall take place on the same day but shall be separate votes in each House.

          (3) If either House votes against the continuation of the monarchy by simple majority of members present and voting, the monarchy is abolished with effect from the date of the vote.

          (4) If both Houses vote in favour of the continuation of the monarchy, succession proceeds in accordance with section 20.

          (5) During the period between the death of the Monarch and the vote under subsection (1), the Heir Apparent does not accede to the throne.

          (6) The Speaker of the House of Commons and the Lord Speaker must certify the result of the votes under this section and publish the certification immediately.
      - title: "Interim President"
        text: |
          (1) If the monarchy is abolished under section 17(3), a joint sitting of both Houses of Parliament must, within 7 days, elect an Interim President.

          (2) The Interim President must be—

          - (a) a serving Member of the House of Commons; or
          - (b) a serving member of the House of Lords.

          (3) The election shall be by simple majority of members present and voting at the joint sitting.

          (3A) If no candidate receives a simple majority in the first round of voting—

          - (a) the two candidates with the highest number of votes proceed to a second round;
          - (b) the second round shall take place immediately following the first round;
          - (c) the candidate receiving the simple majority of votes in the second round is elected.

          (3B) The Speaker of the House of Commons shall preside over the joint sitting and determine the procedure for—

          - (a) nomination of candidates (which must require support of at least 20 members);
          - (b) conduct of the ballot (which must be by secret ballot);
          - (c) announcement of results.

          (4) The Interim President shall exercise the constitutional functions previously performed by the Monarch, including—

          - (a) certifying Acts of Parliament in accordance with section 11;
          - (b) appointing the Prime Minister following a vote of Parliament;
          - (c) representing the state on ceremonial occasions;
          - (d) such other functions as were previously performed by the Monarch and are specified by resolution of both Houses.

          (5) The Interim President shall be paid—

          - (a) a salary of £250,000 per annum, increased annually in line with the Consumer Prices Index;
          - (b) subject to income tax and national insurance contributions.

          (6) The Interim President shall be provided with—

          - (a) appropriate office accommodation;
          - (b) security protection;
          - (c) administrative support from the Cabinet Office.

          (7) The Interim President holds office until—

          - (a) legislation establishing permanent constitutional arrangements comes into force; or
          - (b) they resign or are removed by vote of both Houses.

          (8) The Interim President may not simultaneously hold ministerial office.
      - title: "Consequences of abolition vote"
        text: |
          (1) If the monarchy is abolished under section 17(3), the following shall occur with immediate effect—

          - (a) the former Monarch (if the vote concerns succession after death) and all members of the Royal Family lose all entitlements under this Act;
          - (b) salaries under section 8 cease;
          - (c) residence rights under section 3 cease;
          - (d) security provision provided at public expense ceases, subject to subsection (3);
          - (e) employment by the Royal Household under section 10 terminates.

          (2) The person who was the Heir Apparent immediately before the abolition vote must vacate Buckingham Palace immediately.

          (3) Transitional arrangements apply as follows—

          - (a) each person who was in receipt of a salary under section 8 or employment under section 10 immediately before abolition receives a payment equivalent to 6 months' salary;
          - (b) security protection continues for 6 months for persons who were entitled to it immediately before abolition;
          - (c) the Royal Household continues to exist for 6 months to wind up its affairs and shall then be dissolved.

          (4) Members of the former Royal Family may continue to use courtesy titles (such as King, Queen, Prince, Princess, Duke, or other titles) but such titles—

          - (a) carry no legal status;
          - (b) confer no privileges, exemptions, or entitlements;
          - (c) do not affect their status as private citizens subject to all laws.

          (5) Private property of members of the former Royal Family, including Balmoral Castle and Sandringham House, remains their property.

          (6) All Crown Estate property remains in public ownership and is not affected by the abolition of the monarchy.

          (7) The Secretary of State must, within 6 months of the abolition vote, publish a White Paper setting out proposals for permanent constitutional arrangements.

          (8) The Secretary of State must, within 12 months of the abolition vote, introduce legislation to establish permanent constitutional arrangements to replace the abolished monarchy.

          (9) In preparing the White Paper under subsection (7), the Secretary of State must—

          - (a) have due regard to public opinion on constitutional arrangements;
          - (b) consider whether a referendum on the proposed arrangements would be appropriate;
          - (c) if deciding not to hold a referendum, publish reasons for that decision.
      - title: "Continuation of monarchy"
        text: |
          (1) If both Houses vote in favour of the continuation of the monarchy under section 17, the Heir Apparent accedes to the throne immediately upon certification of the votes.

          (2) Upon accession, the new Monarch becomes entitled to—

          - (a) the salary specified in section 8(1);
          - (b) residence rights under section 3;
          - (c) all other entitlements under this Act applying to the Monarch.

          (3) If the new Monarch was previously in receipt of the Heir Apparent's salary under section 8(2), that salary ceases upon accession.

          (4) The person who becomes Heir Apparent upon the accession becomes entitled to the salary specified in section 8(2).

          (5) All provisions of this Act apply to the new Monarch in the same manner as they applied to their predecessor.

          (6) The accession does not affect—

          - (a) the functions or budget of the Royal Household;
          - (b) the management of the Crown Estate;
          - (c) the removal of exemptions under Part 3.
  - title: "Commencement and Transitional Provisions"
    clauses:
      - title: "Commencement"
        text: |
          (1) This Act comes into force immediately upon receiving Royal Assent.

          (2) Section 11(6) provides that the grant of Royal Assent to this Act shall be the final occasion on which Royal Assent is given.

          (3) All provisions of this Act have effect from commencement unless otherwise specified.
      - title: "Transitional arrangements"
        text: |
          (1) The transfers under section 1 shall be completed within 6 months of commencement or as soon as reasonably practicable thereafter.

          (2) The Royal Household established under section 9 shall be fully operational as an executive non-departmental public body within 6 months of commencement.

          (3) During the transitional period specified in subsections (1) and (2)—

          - (a) the existing Royal Household may continue to operate;
          - (b) the Crown Estate Commissioners may take such steps as are necessary to prepare for the transfers under section 1;
          - (c) the Deputy Prime Minister may issue directions to facilitate the establishment of the Royal Household as a public body.

          (4) The Deputy Prime Minister must, within 3 months of commencement, appoint the Chair and members of the Royal Household board in accordance with section 9(8).

          (5) Any property, rights, or liabilities that cannot reasonably be transferred within the period specified in subsection (1) shall transfer as soon as reasonably practicable thereafter, and the Crown Estate must report to Parliament every 3 months on progress.
      - title: "Current obligations and contracts"
        text: |
          (1) All commitments under the Sovereign Grant for the financial year in which this Act commences shall be honoured from—

          - (a) remaining Sovereign Grant funds; and
          - (b) the budget of the Royal Household established under section 9 to the extent that Sovereign Grant funds are insufficient.

          (2) The refurbishment of Buckingham Palace ongoing at commencement shall continue and be funded from—

          - (a) the budget of the Royal Household for works to working spaces and public areas;
          - (b) the Crown Estate budget for works to the fabric and structure of the building.

          (3) All contracts, agreements, and legal obligations entered into by—

          - (a) the Duchy of Lancaster;
          - (b) the Duchy of Cornwall;
          - (c) the Royal Household in its previous form;
          - (d) the Crown Estate;

          continue in force with the successor bodies under this Act as parties, without requiring novation or consent of counterparties.

          (4) Any legal proceedings to which the Duchy of Lancaster, Duchy of Cornwall, or previous Royal Household is a party may be continued by or against the Crown Estate or Royal Household (as appropriate) without abatement.
      - title: "Staff transfers"
        text: |
          (1) Staff employed by—

          - (a) the Duchy of Lancaster;
          - (b) the Duchy of Cornwall;
          - (c) the Royal Household in its previous form;

          immediately before commencement shall transfer to the employment of the Crown Estate or the Royal Household established under section 9, as appropriate to their functions.

          (2) The Transfer of Undertakings (Protection of Employment) Regulations 2006 apply to transfers under subsection (1).

          (3) The Crown Estate and the Royal Household must—

          - (a) inform affected staff of the transfers and their implications;
          - (b) consult with any recognised trade unions;
          - (c) ensure continuity of employment terms where required by law.

          (4) No member of staff shall be dismissed or have their terms and conditions worsened by reason only of the transfer, except as permitted by the regulations specified in subsection (2).
  - title: "Interpretation and Final Provisions"
    clauses:
      - title: "Definitions"
        text: |
          (1) In this Act—

          **"Crown Estate"** means the Crown Estate established by the Crown Estate Act 1961, as amended by this Act;

          **"Heir Apparent"** means—

          - (a) the eldest child of the Monarch; or
          - (b) where the Monarch has no children, the person first in the line of succession to the Crown in accordance with the Act of Settlement 1701 and the Succession to the Crown Act 2013;

          **"long-term partner"** means a person with whom the Monarch or Heir Apparent shares their primary residence;

          **"Monarch"** means the person holding the office of King or Queen under the Act of Settlement 1701 and the Succession to the Crown Act 2013;

          **"member of the Royal Family"** means—

          - (a) the Monarch;
          - (b) the spouse or long-term partner of the Monarch;
          - (c) the Heir Apparent;
          - (d) the spouse or long-term partner of the Heir Apparent;
          - (e) any child of the Monarch or Heir Apparent under the age of 18;
          - (f) any other person entitled to the style of Royal Highness;
          - (g) any person in the line of succession to the Crown;

          **"official duties"** means—

          - (a) the State Opening of Parliament;
          - (b) state visits and state banquets;
          - (c) investitures and honours ceremonies;
          - (d) garden parties hosted on behalf of His Majesty's Government;
          - (e) other activities undertaken at the request of His Majesty's Government and organised by or on behalf of the Government;

          and does not include private charitable patronages, personal appearances, or activities not organised by or on behalf of His Majesty's Government;

          **"public event"** means any official duty as defined in this section or any event organised by or on behalf of His Majesty's Government;

          **"Royal Household"** means the body corporate established by section 9.

          (2) References to the Monarch include, where the context requires, references to a former Monarch after abdication or after a vote for abolition of the monarchy.

          (3) References to market rate mean the rate that would be charged for equivalent use by an arm's length party in the open market.
      - title: "Consequential amendments and repeals"
        text: |
          (1) Schedule 4 contains consequential amendments to primary and secondary legislation.

          (2) Schedule 5 contains repeals.

          (3) The Secretary of State may by regulations make such further amendments to any enactment as appear necessary or expedient in consequence of this Act.

          (4) Regulations under subsection (3) may include transitional, transitory, or saving provision.

          (5) Regulations under subsection (3) that amend primary legislation are subject to the affirmative procedure.

          (6) Other regulations under subsection (3) are subject to the negative procedure.
      - title: "Extent"
        text: |
          (1) This Act extends to England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland.

          (2) Amendments and repeals made by this Act have the same extent as the provisions to which they relate.

          (3) The consent of the Scottish Parliament is required for provisions of this Act that modify the legislative competence of that Parliament or the executive competence of the Scottish Ministers.

          (4) The consent of Senedd Cymru is required for provisions of this Act that modify the legislative competence of that body or the executive competence of the Welsh Ministers.

          (5) The consent of the Northern Ireland Assembly is required for provisions of this Act that would, if contained in an Act of that Assembly, be within the legislative competence of that Assembly.
      - title: "Short title"
        text: |
          This Act may be cited as the Crown Reform Act 2026.

## Introduction

1. These Explanatory Notes relate to the Crown Reform Bill. They have been prepared by Purple to assist the reader of the Bill and to help inform debate on it. They do not form part of the Bill and have not been endorsed by Parliament.

2. These Notes should be read in conjunction with the Bill. They are not, and are not meant to be, a comprehensive description of the Bill.

## Summary

{:start="3"}
3. The Crown Reform Bill makes comprehensive provision for the reform of the monarchy and Crown property. It transfers the Duchies of Lancaster and Cornwall and the Royal Collection into public ownership. It abolishes the Sovereign Grant and provides for payment of salaries to the Monarch and Heir Apparent. It removes legal exemptions previously applying to the Crown and Royal Family. It abolishes Royal Assent, King's Consent, and the Privy Council. It requires publication of communications between the Monarch and Ministers. It provides for parliamentary votes on the continuation of the monarchy upon each succession.

## Policy background

{:start="4"}
4. The Bill implements a policy of transforming the constitutional and financial arrangements concerning the monarchy. Key policy objectives include:
   - Bringing Crown property into full public ownership and control
   - Ending financial exemptions and replacing the Sovereign Grant with fixed salaries
   - Ensuring the monarchy is fully subject to the law without exemption
   - Removing the monarchy from the legislative process
   - Providing democratic accountability for the continuation of the institution
   - Preventing nepotism in royal employment through public interest tests

## Legal background

{:start="5"}
5. The Bill makes significant changes to constitutional arrangements that have developed over centuries. It repeals the Sovereign Grant Act 2011 and makes consequential amendments to numerous other enactments. The abolition of Royal Assent is particularly significant as this has been a requirement for legislation since medieval times, though it has not been refused since 1708.

## Territorial extent and application

{:start="6"}
6. The Bill extends to England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland. Legislative consent motions are required from the Scottish Parliament, Senedd Cymru, and Northern Ireland Assembly for provisions affecting devolved competences.

## Commentary on clauses

### Part 1 — Property and Crown Estate

{:start="7"}
7. Clause 1 provides for the automatic transfer of the Duchies of Lancaster and Cornwall, the Royal Collection, and other Crown property to the Crown Estate. This occurs by operation of law without requiring any conveyancing formalities. All existing contracts and legal obligations continue with the Crown Estate as successor.

8. Clause 2 places the Crown Estate within HM Treasury and provides that all revenue goes to the Consolidated Fund (this is already the current position). The Crown Estate may continue using the Duchy brands commercially.

9. Clause 3 provides that the Monarch may reside at Buckingham Palace without charge. This is tied accommodation analogous to the Prime Minister's residence at 10 Downing Street. No other members of the Royal Family have residence entitlements.

10. Clause 4 requires payment at market rate for any use of Crown Estate property by the Royal Family that is not for official duties. Market rate is determined by independent valuation.

11. Clause 5 confirms that private property including Balmoral and Sandringham is not affected and remains privately owned, but becomes subject to normal taxation.

### Part 2 — Taxation and Remuneration

{:start="12"}
12. Clause 6 removes all tax exemptions applying to the Monarch and Royal Family. They become liable to all forms of taxation on the same basis as any other person. The provision is not retrospective.

13. Clause 7 abolishes the Sovereign Grant Act 2011. The Sovereign Grant, set at £132 million for both 2025-26 and 2026-27, ceases.

14. Clause 8 provides for fixed salaries of £750,000 for the Monarch and £150,000 for the Heir Apparent, increased annually in line with the Consumer Prices Index. These salaries are subject to tax.

15. Clause 9 establishes the Royal Household as an executive non-departmental public body sponsored by the Deputy Prime Minister. It is subject to full parliamentary and audit oversight and its staff are civil servants.

16. Clause 10 allows other members of the Royal Family to be employed by the Royal Household for official government work only, but only where this is in the public interest and approved by the Royal Household board. The clause includes safeguards against nepotism by requiring consideration of whether non-royal candidates could perform the duties, whether the royal status provides unique benefits, and cost-effectiveness. The Royal Household must publish quarterly justifications for such employment. Employees are paid on Civil Service scales. Private charitable work is not funded.

### Part 3 — Legislative Scrutiny and Legal Exemptions

{:start="17"}
17. Clause 11 abolishes Royal Assent. Bills become Acts upon certification by the Speakers that they have passed through Parliament. This represents a fundamental change to the legislative process.

18. Clause 12 abolishes King's Consent, which currently requires the Monarch's consent before Parliament may debate bills affecting royal interests.

19. Clause 13 abolishes the Privy Council. Its functions transfer to ministers. Orders in Council are approved by the relevant Secretary of State without monarchical involvement.

20. Clause 14 confirms the Monarch has the same access to legislation as any member of the public and no special rights to view draft bills or request changes outside the consultation process.

21. Clause 15 requires publication of communications between the Monarch and Ministers on legislation and policy within 14 days. Each publication must state that the Monarch's views carry no legal weight. The weekly PM audience remains private.

22. Clause 16 removes all statutory exemptions applying to the Crown and Royal Family. They become fully subject to all legislation. Schedule 2 lists key affected legislation but the provision is general.

### Part 4 — Succession and Parliamentary Abolition

{:start="23"}
23. Clause 17 provides that within 28 days of the Monarch's death, both Houses must vote on whether the monarchy should continue. The extended period allows for proper debate whilst maintaining prompt decision-making. A vote against continuation by either House abolishes the monarchy.

24. Clause 18 provides for election of an Interim President if the monarchy is abolished. The election uses a runoff system if no candidate achieves majority in the first round. Nominations require support from at least 20 members to prevent frivolous candidacies. The ballot is secret to allow members to vote according to conscience. This is a temporary role until permanent constitutional arrangements are established.

25. Clause 19 sets out the consequences of abolition, including immediate cessation of all entitlements, transitional payments and security, and requirements for the Government to publish a White Paper and introduce legislation within specified timeframes. The White Paper must have due regard to public opinion and consider whether a referendum is appropriate, though this is not mandated.

26. Clause 20 provides that if both Houses vote for continuation, succession proceeds normally with all provisions of the Act applying to the new Monarch.

### Part 5 — Commencement and Transitional Provisions

{:start="27"}
27. Clause 21 provides for immediate commencement upon Royal Assent, which will be the final use of Royal Assent before its abolition.

28. Clauses 22–24 provide for transitional arrangements including 6-month periods to complete property transfers and establish the Royal Household, continuation of current commitments including the Buckingham Palace refurbishment, and TUPE protection for transferring staff.

### Part 6 — Interpretation and Final Provisions

{:start="29"}
29. Clause 25 provides definitions of key terms including "official duties" which is limited to state occasions and government-organised events, excluding private charitable work.

30. Clauses 26–28 provide for consequential amendments (Schedules 4 and 5), extent to all parts of the UK, and the short title.

## Financial implications

{:start="31"}
31. The Bill has significant financial implications:
   - **Revenue**: All Crown Estate revenue continues to flow to the Consolidated Fund as at present. The Bill does not change this flow. The amount varies from year to year and fell sharply in 2025-26 as offshore wind income declined. Tax revenue from the Royal Family is estimated at £2–5 million annually depending on their private income.
   - **Expenditure**: The Sovereign Grant (£132 million in 2026-27) is abolished. It is replaced by salaries totalling approximately £900,000 and a budget for the Royal Household estimated at £30–50 million annually (significantly less than the current Sovereign Grant).
   - **Net saving**: Approximately £81–101 million annually to the public purse.
   - **One-off costs**: Establishing the Royal Household as an NDPB and completing property transfers may cost £5–10 million in transitional expenses.

## Impact on existing legislation

{:start="32"}
32. The Bill repeals the Sovereign Grant Act 2011 entirely and makes extensive amendments to constitutional and tax legislation. Schedule 4 lists major consequential amendments. The Secretary of State has power to make further consequential amendments by regulations.

## Parliamentary procedure

{:start="33"}
33. The Bill will follow the normal parliamentary procedure for constitutional legislation. It requires Royal Assent (for the final time) to become law. Legislative consent motions are required from devolved legislatures.
