key: sex-work-bill
title: Sex Work (Decriminalisation and Worker Protection) Bill
long_title: A Bill to decriminalise sex work between consenting adults; to provide for the disregard and deletion of historical convictions and cautions for offences relating to sex work that have been abolished; to prohibit financial discrimination against sex workers; to provide for the licensing of sex worker cooperative venues; to create offences of exploitative extraction from sex work; to define worker-owned content platforms for the purposes of levy exemption under the Finance Act and government investment eligibility; to make consequential amendments to existing legislation; and for connected purposes.
policies: []
parts:
- title: Decriminalisation
  clauses:
  - num: '1'
    title: Abolition of offences relating to sex work
    text: |
      (1) The following offences are abolished with effect from the commencement of this Act—

      - (a) the offence of loitering or soliciting for the purposes of prostitution under section 1 of the Street Offences Act 1959;
      - (b) the offence of soliciting under section 51A of the Sexual Offences Act 2003;
      - (c) any offence under subordinate legislation relating to the offering or advertising of sexual services by the person who is to provide those services.

      (2) No person may be prosecuted, convicted, or sentenced for an offence specified in subsection (1) in respect of conduct occurring on or after the commencement of this Act.

      (3) Schedule 1 sets out the full list of offences abolished by this Part.
  - num: '2'
    title: Abolition of offences relating to purchase of sexual services
    text: |
      (1) The offence of paying for sexual services of a prostitute subjected to force under section 53A of the Sexual Offences Act 2003 is repealed.

      (2) No offence is committed by a person who pays for, or offers to pay for, sexual services provided by a consenting adult, whether that adult is working alone, in a cooperative, or in any other lawful arrangement.

      (3) Nothing in this section—

      - (a) affects the law relating to trafficking in persons;
      - (b) affects the law relating to sexual exploitation of persons under 18;
      - (c) creates any obligation on any person to provide sexual services.
  - num: '3'
    title: Abolition of brothel-keeping offences
    text: |
      (1) The following offences are abolished with effect from the commencement of this Act—

      - (a) the offence of keeping a brothel under section 33 of the Sexual Offences Act 1956;
      - (b) the offence of keeping a brothel used for prostitution under section 33A of that Act;
      - (c) the offence of a landlord letting premises for use as a brothel under section 34 of that Act;
      - (d) the offence of a tenant permitting premises to be used as a brothel under section 35 of that Act.

      (2) Premises used by two or more persons for the provision of sexual services by consenting adults are not, by reason only of that use, unlawful.

      (3) Subsection (2) is subject to—

      - (a) the licensing requirements under Part 4 where the premises are operated as a Sex Worker Cooperative;
      - (b) the anti-extraction offences under Part 5;
      - (c) the law relating to trafficking and the sexual exploitation of minors.
  - num: '4'
    title: 'Saving: trafficking and slavery offences'
    text: |
      (1) Nothing in this Part affects—

      - (a) any offence under the Modern Slavery Act 2015;
      - (b) any offence under the Sexual Offences Act 2003 relating to causing or inciting prostitution for gain where the person exploited is subject to force, threats, deception, or coercion;
      - (c) any other offence involving force, threats, fraud, or coercion in connection with sexual activity.

      (2) For the avoidance of doubt, the decriminalisation of sex work under this Part does not constitute evidence that a person was not trafficked or coerced, and does not affect the admissibility of any evidence in proceedings under the Modern Slavery Act 2015.
  - num: '5'
    title: 'Saving: offences involving persons under 18'
    text: |
      (1) Nothing in this Part affects any offence involving sexual activity with or by a person under the age of 18.

      (2) The offences abolished by this Part apply only in respect of sexual services provided by and between persons aged 18 or over.

      (3) Where any doubt arises as to whether a person was aged 18 or over at the time of any conduct, the burden of proof lies on the person asserting that they were.
- title: 'Historical Convictions: Disregard and Deletion'
  clauses:
  - num: '6'
    title: Automatic disregard of qualifying convictions and cautions
    text: |
      (1) A conviction or caution for a qualifying offence is automatically disregarded by virtue of this section with effect from the commencement of this Part.

      (2) A disregard under this section applies without any requirement for application by the person concerned.

      (3) A disregard under this section applies whether or not the person concerned is aware of it.

      (4) "Qualifying offence" has the meaning given in section 7.

      (5) A disregard under this section does not apply where the conviction or caution is excluded under section 8.
  - num: '7'
    title: Meaning of qualifying offence
    text: |
      (1) A "qualifying offence" is a conviction or caution for any offence listed in Schedule 2.

      (2) Schedule 2 lists—

      - (a) the offences abolished by Part 1 of this Act; and
      - (b) any other offence under any enactment relating to the offering, provision, or advertisement of sexual services by the person who is to provide those services, where the conduct would not have been an offence had this Act been in force at the time.

      (3) The Secretary of State may by regulations amend Schedule 2 to add further offences.

      (4) Regulations under subsection (3) are subject to the affirmative procedure.
  - num: '8'
    title: Exclusions from disregard
    text: |
      (1) A conviction or caution is not a qualifying offence, and is not disregarded under section 6, if—

      - (a) the same proceedings in which the conviction was obtained also resulted in a conviction for—
        - (i) any offence under the Modern Slavery Act 2015;
        - (ii) any offence relating to the sexual exploitation of a person under 18;
        - (iii) any offence of causing or controlling prostitution for gain under the Sexual Offences Act 2003 where the person exploited was subject to force, threats, or coercion; or
      - (b) the caution was given in circumstances where the conduct also involved the exploitation of another person by force, threats, deception, or coercion.

      (2) Where a conviction covers multiple counts and only some counts relate to qualifying offences, the disregard applies only to those counts relating to qualifying offences. The remainder of the conviction is unaffected.

      (3) Where there is a dispute as to whether a conviction or caution falls within subsection (1), the person concerned may apply to the Secretary of State under section 12 for a certificate of disregard, and the Secretary of State must determine the question under section 13.
  - num: '9'
    title: Effect of disregard
    text: |
      (1) Where a conviction or caution is disregarded under section 6—

      - (a) the person is to be treated for all purposes in law as if they had never been convicted of or cautioned for the offence;
      - (b) the conviction or caution is not to be disclosed by the person in response to any question about their criminal record, however framed;
      - (c) a failure to disclose a disregarded conviction or caution does not give rise to any liability, civil or criminal;
      - (d) any question asking whether a person has ever been convicted of or cautioned for an offence is to be treated as not referring to any disregarded conviction or caution.

      (2) The effect of subsection (1) applies in particular in relation to—

      - (a) Disclosure and Barring Service checks, including standard and enhanced DBS certificates;
      - (b) applications for employment or voluntary work;
      - (c) applications for housing, including social housing;
      - (d) applications for credit or financial services;
      - (e) immigration and nationality proceedings, in accordance with section 11;
      - (f) any legal proceedings in which criminal record is relevant.

      (3) A person who unlawfully discloses a disregarded conviction or caution commits an offence.

      (4) A person convicted of an offence under subsection (3)—

      - (a) on indictment, is liable to a fine; and
      - (b) summarily, is liable to a fine not exceeding the statutory maximum.
  - num: '10'
    title: Deletion of records
    text: |
      (1) The Secretary of State must, within 6 months of the commencement of this Part, take all steps reasonably practicable to ensure that—

      - (a) the Police National Computer is updated to remove all records of qualifying convictions and cautions that are disregarded under section 6;
      - (b) the Disclosure and Barring Service database is updated accordingly;
      - (c) records held by the National DNA Database, fingerprint databases, and any other biometric databases are deleted where the only basis for retention was a qualifying conviction or caution.

      (2) The Secretary of State must notify the Information Commissioner's Office of the deletions carried out under subsection (1).

      (3) A person who believes that records relating to their disregarded conviction or caution have not been deleted may apply to the Secretary of State, who must investigate and take corrective action within 28 days.

      (4) The Secretary of State must publish an annual report for the first 5 years after commencement of this Part setting out—

      - (a) the number of records deleted under subsection (1);
      - (b) applications received under subsection (3) and action taken.
  - num: '11'
    title: 'Immigration and nationality: effect of disregard'
    text: |
      (1) A disregarded conviction or caution under section 6 has no effect in any immigration or nationality proceedings, including—

      - (a) applications for leave to remain or indefinite leave to remain;
      - (b) applications for naturalisation as a British citizen;
      - (c) deportation proceedings;
      - (d) any other proceedings under the Immigration Acts.

      (2) A disregarded conviction or caution is not a "criminal conviction" for the purposes of any provision of the Immigration Rules or the Nationality and Borders Act 2022.

      (3) A Home Office decision that treats a disregarded conviction or caution as relevant to immigration status is unlawful and may be challenged by judicial review.

      (4) Any person who has been refused immigration status or deported where a qualifying conviction or caution was a material factor in that decision may apply to the Secretary of State to have their case reviewed, and the Secretary of State must determine such applications within 6 months.
  - num: '12'
    title: Application for certificate of disregard
    text: |
      (1) A person may apply to the Secretary of State for a certificate confirming that a specified conviction or caution is disregarded under section 6.

      (2) An application under subsection (1) may be made where—

      - (a) the person requires confirmation for a specific purpose; or
      - (b) the person disputes that an exclusion under section 8 applies to their conviction or caution.

      (3) An application must be made in writing and must specify—

      - (a) the offence in respect of which the certificate is sought;
      - (b) the date of the conviction or caution;
      - (c) the court or police force by which the conviction was recorded or the caution administered; and
      - (d) the purpose for which the certificate is required, if applicable.

      (4) The Secretary of State must determine an application within 60 days of receipt.

      (5) A certificate issued under this section is conclusive evidence that the conviction or caution is disregarded for all purposes.

      (6) No fee may be charged for an application under this section.
  - num: '13'
    title: Review by the Secretary of State
    text: |
      (1) Where a person applies under section 12(2)(b) on the ground that an exclusion under section 8 does not apply to their conviction or caution, the Secretary of State must—

      - (a) obtain the relevant court records and police records;
      - (b) consider written representations from the applicant; and
      - (c) determine whether the exclusion under section 8 applies.

      (2) If the Secretary of State determines that the exclusion does not apply, a certificate of disregard must be issued and steps taken under section 10 to delete the relevant records.

      (3) If the Secretary of State determines that the exclusion does apply, the applicant must be given written reasons and notified of their right to challenge the decision by judicial review.

      (4) The Secretary of State must have regard to the principle that doubt should be resolved in favour of the applicant.
- title: Financial Non-Discrimination
  clauses:
  - num: '14'
    title: Prohibition on financial discrimination
    text: |
      (1) A financial institution must not refuse to provide, restrict, or withdraw a financial service to a person solely or principally on the grounds that the person's income derives wholly or partly from lawful sex work.

      (2) For the purposes of this section, "financial service" includes—

      - (a) the opening or maintenance of a bank or building society account;
      - (b) the provision of payment processing services;
      - (c) the provision of credit or lending facilities;
      - (d) the provision of insurance;
      - (e) any other service regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority.

      (3) "Financial institution" means—

      - (a) a bank or building society authorised under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000;
      - (b) a payment institution authorised or registered under the Payment Services Regulations 2017;
      - (c) any other person providing financial services regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority.

      (4) Nothing in this section—

      - (a) prevents a financial institution from refusing services on grounds of money laundering, fraud, or other criminal activity unrelated to the lawful nature of sex work;
      - (b) requires a financial institution to provide services where the refusal is based on a ground that would apply equally to persons not engaged in sex work;
      - (c) affects any obligation under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 or the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 in relation to genuinely unlawful activity.
  - num: '15'
    title: Enforcement by the Financial Conduct Authority
    text: |
      (1) The Financial Conduct Authority has responsibility for enforcing section 14.

      (2) A financial institution that contravenes section 14 commits a breach of a requirement imposed by the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and is subject to the FCA's enforcement and disciplinary powers under that Act.

      (3) Without limiting the generality of subsection (2), the FCA may—

      - (a) impose a financial penalty not exceeding 4% of the institution's annual UK revenue;
      - (b) require the institution to restore service to an affected person;
      - (c) publish details of the breach and any penalty imposed;
      - (d) take such other action as the FCA considers appropriate.

      (4) The FCA must publish guidance on the application of section 14 within 6 months of the commencement of this Part.

      (5) The FCA must report annually to Parliament on—

      - (a) the number of complaints received relating to section 14;
      - (b) enforcement action taken;
      - (c) financial penalties imposed.
  - num: '16'
    title: Remedies for financial discrimination
    text: |
      (1) A person who has suffered loss as a result of a contravention of section 14 may bring civil proceedings in the county court or High Court.

      (2) Remedies available in proceedings under subsection (1) include—

      - (a) damages, including for financial loss and distress;
      - (b) an injunction requiring restoration of service;
      - (c) a declaration that the financial institution has contravened section 14.

      (3) Proceedings under this section must be brought within 3 years of the date on which the person became aware, or ought reasonably to have become aware, of the contravention.

      (4) The bringing of proceedings under this section does not prevent the FCA from taking enforcement action under section 15 in respect of the same conduct.
  - num: '17'
    title: Guidance
    text: |
      (1) The Secretary of State may issue guidance to financial institutions on compliance with this Part.

      (2) Guidance under subsection (1) is not legally binding but a financial institution's failure to follow it is admissible as evidence in FCA enforcement proceedings and civil proceedings under section 16.

      (3) Before issuing guidance under subsection (1), the Secretary of State must consult—

      - (a) the Financial Conduct Authority;
      - (b) representatives of sex workers;
      - (c) financial industry representatives.
- title: Sex Worker Cooperative Framework
  clauses:
  - num: '18'
    title: 'Sex Worker Cooperative: definition'
    text: |
      (1) A "Sex Worker Cooperative" is a body that—

      - (a) is established for the purpose of enabling its members to provide sexual services collectively from shared premises;
      - (b) is owned and controlled exclusively by its active worker members in accordance with section 20;
      - (c) holds a licence granted under section 19; and
      - (d) meets the governance requirements set out in section 20 and the licensing standards in Schedule 3.

      (2) A body is not a Sex Worker Cooperative if—

      - (a) any person who is not an active worker member holds any ownership interest, voting right, or entitlement to profit; or
      - (b) it is controlled directly or indirectly by a person who is not an active worker member.

      (3) "Active worker member" means a person who—

      - (a) is aged 18 or over;
      - (b) personally provides sexual services as a substantial part of their work within the cooperative; and
      - (c) has been accepted as a member in accordance with the cooperative's rules.
  - num: '19'
    title: Licensing of Sex Worker Cooperatives
    text: |
      (1) A Sex Worker Cooperative must hold a licence granted by the local authority for the area in which its premises are situated.

      (2) An application for a licence must be made to the relevant local authority and must include—

      - (a) the address of the premises;
      - (b) the names and addresses of all members;
      - (c) the cooperative's rules, including governance arrangements;
      - (d) a statement that all members are active worker members within the meaning of section 18(3);
      - (e) such other information as the local authority may reasonably require.

      (3) A local authority must grant a licence unless satisfied that—

      - (a) the premises do not meet the minimum standards set out in Schedule 3;
      - (b) the applicant has provided false or misleading information; or
      - (c) one or more members are disqualified from membership by reason of a relevant conviction under subsection (5).

      (4) A licence—

      - (a) is granted for a period of 3 years and may be renewed on application;
      - (b) may be granted subject to conditions relating to the standards in Schedule 3;
      - (c) is not transferable.

      (5) A person is disqualified from membership of a licensed cooperative if they have been convicted of—

      - (a) any offence under the Modern Slavery Act 2015;
      - (b) any offence relating to the sexual exploitation of a person under 18; or
      - (c) any offence under Part 5 of this Act.

      (6) The Secretary of State must publish national minimum licensing standards within 6 months of commencement of this Part, having consulted local authorities and representatives of sex workers.

      (7) Local authorities may charge a reasonable fee for the grant and renewal of a licence, not to exceed the costs of administering the licensing function.
  - num: '20'
    title: Governance requirements
    text: |
      (1) A Sex Worker Cooperative must adopt and comply with rules providing for—

      - (a) one member, one vote on all significant decisions of the cooperative, including—
        - (i) pricing of services;
        - (ii) working hours and conditions;
        - (iii) admission and expulsion of members;
        - (iv) distribution of revenue and profit;
        - (v) appointment and removal of any managers or officers;
      - (b) equal entitlement of all members to share in the profits of the cooperative in proportion to their participation in its work, or in such other manner as members unanimously agree;
      - (c) a right of any member to resign from the cooperative on reasonable notice without financial penalty; and
      - (d) a prohibition on any member being required to perform any particular act or provide any particular service against their will.

      (2) No rule of a cooperative may—

      - (a) vest control of the cooperative in any person who is not an active worker member;
      - (b) allow any person to take a share of revenue or profit by virtue of any role other than as an active worker member; or
      - (c) impose financial penalties on members for exercising their right to refuse to provide services.

      (3) The cooperative must hold at least one general meeting of all members in each calendar year at which accounts are presented and members may raise any matter concerning the operation of the cooperative.
  - num: '21'
    title: Ancillary services at market rate
    text: |
      (1) A Sex Worker Cooperative may enter into contracts for ancillary services with persons who are not members of the cooperative.

      (2) "Ancillary services" means services genuinely necessary for the operation of the cooperative's premises, including—

      - (a) cleaning and maintenance;
      - (b) security;
      - (c) booking and scheduling software;
      - (d) accountancy and legal services;
      - (e) laundry; and
      - (f) such other services as the Secretary of State may specify by regulations.

      (3) The price paid for ancillary services must not exceed the market rate for equivalent services provided to comparable non-cooperative premises.

      (4) "Market rate" means the rate that would be charged by an arm's length supplier in the open market, as determined by independent valuation where disputed.

      (5) A contract for ancillary services that requires payment above market rate is void to the extent of the excess.

      (6) Where a cooperative considers that it may be paying above market rate for any ancillary service, it may apply to the local authority for an independent valuation at the authority's expense.

      (7) A person who enters into or enforces a contract for ancillary services knowing that the price exceeds market rate commits an offence under section 27.
  - num: '22'
    title: Employment within cooperatives
    text: |
      (1) A Sex Worker Cooperative may employ persons who are not active worker members to perform support functions that do not include the provision of sexual services.

      (2) Support functions include—

      - (a) reception and administration;
      - (b) security; and
      - (c) cleaning and maintenance.

      (3) Persons employed under subsection (1) must be—

      - (a) paid at least the national living wage;
      - (b) employed under a contract of employment with full employment rights; and
      - (c) not required to participate in or facilitate the provision of sexual services beyond administrative functions.

      (4) A Sex Worker Cooperative may not employ any person as a manager, director, or officer whose functions include control over—

      - (a) the prices charged by members for sexual services;
      - (b) the allocation of work between members; or
      - (c) the terms on which members may work.
  - num: '23'
    title: Publication requirements
    text: |
      (1) A Sex Worker Cooperative must publish annually—

      - (a) its total revenue for the year;
      - (b) total payments to members;
      - (c) total expenditure on ancillary services, identifying each category;
      - (d) the number of active worker members; and
      - (e) a statement that governance requirements under section 20 have been complied with.

      (2) Publication under subsection (1) must be made available to the licensing local authority and to members on request.

      (3) A cooperative is not required to publish the names of its members or any information that would identify individual members.

      (4) The local authority may inspect the accounts and records of a cooperative at any time on reasonable notice for the purpose of verifying compliance with this Part.
  - num: '24'
    title: Suspension and revocation of licences
    text: |
      (1) A local authority may suspend or revoke a licence granted under section 19 where satisfied that—

      - (a) the cooperative is no longer complying with the governance requirements under section 20;
      - (b) the cooperative is paying above market rate for ancillary services in contravention of section 21;
      - (c) a member or employee of the cooperative has been convicted of an offence under Part 5;
      - (d) the cooperative has provided false or misleading information to the local authority; or
      - (e) the premises no longer meet the minimum standards in Schedule 3.

      (2) Before suspending or revoking a licence, the local authority must—

      - (a) give the cooperative written notice of the proposed action and the grounds for it;
      - (b) allow the cooperative at least 28 days to make representations; and
      - (c) consider any representations made.

      (3) A cooperative may appeal a decision to suspend or revoke its licence to a magistrates' court within 21 days of the decision.

      (4) A suspension or revocation does not take effect until any appeal under subsection (3) has been determined or the time for appeal has expired, whichever is earlier.
- title: Anti-Extraction Offences
  clauses:
  - num: '25'
    title: Offence of exploitative extraction from sex work
    text: |
      (1) A person commits an offence if they—

      - (a) receive, or are entitled to receive, a financial benefit derived from another person's sex work; and
      - (b) the receipt does not fall within a permitted category in section 26 and Schedule 4.

      (2) For the purposes of this section—

      - (a) "financial benefit" includes money, money's worth, a share of revenue or profit, a reduction in debt, and any other economic advantage; and
      - (b) "sex work" means the provision of sexual services for payment by a person aged 18 or over acting of their own free will.

      (3) It is a defence for a person charged with an offence under this section to prove on the balance of probabilities that they did not know and had no reasonable grounds to believe that the financial benefit they received was derived from sex work.
  - num: '26'
    title: Permitted categories of financial benefit
    text: |
      (1) The following categories of financial benefit derived from sex work do not constitute an offence under section 25—

      - (a) a sex worker retaining the whole or any part of their own earnings from sex work;
      - (b) a member of a licensed Sex Worker Cooperative receiving a share of revenue distributed in accordance with the cooperative's rules under section 20;
      - (c) a person employed by a Sex Worker Cooperative under section 22 receiving wages for support functions at no more than market rate;
      - (d) a person providing ancillary services to a Sex Worker Cooperative under section 21 at no more than market rate;
      - (e) a person providing ancillary services to an individual sex worker at no more than market rate; and
      - (f) a certified worker-owned content platform under section 32 receiving fees from content creators that are applied in accordance with section 31.

      (2) The permitted categories in subsection (1) are set out in full in Schedule 4, which also provides examples of conduct that does and does not fall within each category.

      (3) The Secretary of State may by regulations amend Schedule 4 to add, remove, or modify permitted categories.

      (4) Regulations under subsection (3) are subject to the affirmative procedure.
  - num: '27'
    title: Exploitation through ancillary services
    text: |
      (1) A person commits an offence under this section if they—

      - (a) provide ancillary services to a sex worker or Sex Worker Cooperative;
      - (b) charge a price that exceeds market rate; and
      - (c) knew or ought reasonably to have known at the time of contracting that—
        - (i) the services were being provided in connection with sex work; and
        - (ii) the price exceeded market rate.

      (2) It is a defence for a person charged under this section to prove on the balance of probabilities that the excess above market rate was due to a documented increase in the cost of providing the service that arose after the contract was entered into.

      (3) For the purposes of this section, "market rate" has the meaning given in section 21(4).
  - num: '28'
    title: Penalties
    text: |
      (1) A person convicted of an offence under section 25 on indictment is liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 14 years, or a fine, or both.

      (2) A person convicted of an offence under section 25 summarily is liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 12 months, or a fine not exceeding the statutory maximum, or both.

      (3) A person convicted of an offence under section 27 on indictment is liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years, or a fine, or both.

      (4) A person convicted of an offence under section 27 summarily is liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 12 months, or a fine not exceeding the statutory maximum, or both.

      (5) The court may, in addition to any penalty under subsections (1) to (4), make a confiscation order under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 in respect of any benefit derived from the offence.
  - num: '29'
    title: Civil recovery
    text: |
      (1) A sex worker who has suffered loss as a result of exploitative extraction may bring civil proceedings to recover that loss independently of any criminal proceedings.

      (2) In civil proceedings under this section the claimant need only prove their case on the balance of probabilities.

      (3) Remedies available include—

      - (a) damages equivalent to the total amount extracted;
      - (b) additional damages not exceeding twice the amount extracted where the court is satisfied the extraction was deliberate; and
      - (c) an injunction restraining further extraction.

      (4) Proceedings under this section must be brought within 6 years of the date on which the extraction occurred, or within 3 years of the date on which the claimant first knew or ought reasonably to have known of the extraction, whichever is later.

      (5) Legal aid is available for proceedings under this section to persons who satisfy the means and merits criteria under the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012, and the Lord Chancellor must have regard to the particular vulnerability of claimants in this context when setting eligibility criteria.
  - num: '30'
    title: Corporate liability
    text: |
      (1) Where an offence under this Part is committed by a body corporate and it is proved that the offence was committed with the consent or connivance of a director, manager, secretary, or other similar officer, that person as well as the body corporate commits the offence and is liable to be proceeded against accordingly.

      (2) Where the affairs of a body corporate are managed by its members, subsection (1) applies in relation to acts or omissions of a member in connection with that management.
- title: Worker-Owned Content Platforms
  clauses:
  - num: '31'
    title: Definition and governance standards
    text: |
      (1) A "worker-owned content platform" is a platform that—

      - (a) operates in the United Kingdom;
      - (b) enables persons to upload, distribute, or monetise content of a sexual nature; and
      - (c) is owned and controlled exclusively by its active content creator members in accordance with this section.

      (2) "Active content creator member" means a person who—

      - (a) is aged 18 or over;
      - (b) personally creates and uploads content to the platform as a substantial part of their use of it; and
      - (c) holds a membership interest in the platform in accordance with its rules.

      (3) A platform is not a worker-owned content platform if—

      - (a) any person who is not an active content creator member holds any ownership interest, voting right, or entitlement to net revenue; or
      - (b) it is controlled directly or indirectly by a person who is not an active content creator member.

      (4) A worker-owned content platform must adopt and comply with rules providing for—

      - (a) one member, one vote on all significant decisions, including platform fees, content moderation policy, distribution of net revenue, and admission and expulsion of members;
      - (b) all net revenue, after deduction of verified operating costs, to be distributed to active content creator members in proportion to their contribution or in such other manner as members unanimously agree;
      - (c) operating costs to be independently verified annually; and
      - (d) a right of any member to leave the platform and take their content with them on reasonable notice.

      (5) A worker-owned content platform must meet the minimum governance requirements in Part 1 of Schedule 5 and the annual reporting requirements in Part 2 of that Schedule.

      (6) "Net revenue" means total platform revenue less—

## Introduction

1. These Explanatory Notes relate to the Sex Work (Decriminalisation and Worker Protection) Bill. They have been prepared to assist the reader of the Bill and to help inform debate. They do not form part of the Bill and have not been endorsed by Parliament.

## Summary

{:start="2"}
2. The Bill does five things. It decriminalises sex work between consenting adults, removing criminal penalties for workers and clients and repealing brothel-keeping offences. It provides for the automatic disregard and deletion of historical convictions and cautions for the offences it abolishes. It prohibits financial institutions from discriminating against sex workers. It creates a cooperative ownership and licensing framework for sex worker-run venues. It creates criminal and civil offences targeting the extraction of financial benefit from another person's sex work. It also defines worker-owned content platforms in terms that qualify them for exemption from the Content Extraction Levy established under the Finance Act.

## Policy background

{:start="3"}
3. The Bill draws on the evidence base from New Zealand's Prostitution Reform Act 2003, which demonstrates that full decriminalisation improves worker safety without increasing trafficking. The Bill's approach to cooperative ownership reflects the Purple Party's production/extraction framework: sex work is production by the worker; third-party extraction of revenue from that work without genuine value exchange is the harm the criminal law should address.
4. The National Income Threshold established under the Social Security (National Income Threshold) Act is the structural foundation of this policy. Where no adult is compelled into sex work by economic necessity, the remaining choices are more genuinely free and more clearly deserving of legal protection.

## Territorial extent and application

{:start="5"}
5. This Act extends to England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland. The provisions relating to decriminalisation, historical conviction disregard, cooperative licensing, and anti-extraction offences (Parts 1, 2, 4, 5, and 7) extend to England and Wales only, as sexual offences and licensing are devolved matters. Financial non-discrimination (Part 3) and worker-owned content platforms (Part 6) extend to the whole of the United Kingdom, as financial services and online regulation are reserved matters. Legislative consent motions are required from the Scottish Parliament and the Northern Ireland Assembly. The Scottish Government and Northern Ireland Executive are invited to introduce equivalent legislation to extend the decriminalisation and historical conviction disregard provisions to those nations.

## Commentary on clauses

### Part 1 — Decriminalisation

{:start="6"}
6. Clauses 1 to 3 abolish the criminal offences that make sex work in practice extremely unsafe: soliciting, purchasing, and brothel-keeping. The Nordic model — criminalising buyers while leaving workers technically legal — is rejected on the evidence that it pushes the market underground and prevents workers from vetting clients openly or working in groups for safety. Schedule 1 lists every offence abolished.
7. Clauses 4 and 5 are the essential savings provisions. Nothing in Part 1 affects trafficking or slavery offences under the Modern Slavery Act 2015, or any offence involving persons under 18. The Bill decriminalises consensual adult sex work only.

### Part 2 — Historical convictions: disregard and deletion

{:start="8"}
8. Clauses 6 to 13 provide for the automatic disregard of historical convictions and cautions for the offences abolished by Part 1. This follows the model of the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012, which provided for the disregard of historical convictions for consensual same-sex offences. A disregard is stronger than a pardon: a pardon forgives the conviction but leaves it on record; a disregard treats the conviction as if it had never occurred for all legal purposes. Clause 9 makes clear that a disregarded conviction need not be disclosed in response to any question, however framed, and that failure to disclose does not give rise to liability.
9. Clause 10 imposes an active duty on the Secretary of State to delete records — from the Police National Computer, the DBS database, and biometric databases — within 6 months of commencement. This is critical: the disregard is meaningless if the conviction remains on databases consulted by employers, landlords, and the Home Office.
10. Clause 11 addresses the particular position of people whose immigration status has been affected by a qualifying conviction. A disregarded conviction is treated as if it never existed in immigration and nationality proceedings, and a review mechanism is provided for people who have previously been refused status or deported where a qualifying conviction was a material factor.
11. Schedule 2 defines the qualifying offences with care. The brothel-keeping offences under sections 33 and 33A of the Sexual Offences Act 1956 qualify only where the convicted person was themselves a sex worker working from the premises — not where the person was exploiting others. The notes in Schedule 2 Part 2 and the doubt-resolution principle in clause 13(4) ensure that ambiguous cases are resolved in favour of the applicant.

### Part 3 — Financial non-discrimination

{:start="12"}
12. Clauses 14 to 17 address the practical exclusion of sex workers from the financial system. Banks and payment processors routinely debank sex workers despite the lawful status of their work. Without a bank account a worker cannot take card payments, pay tax, access credit, or build financial resilience. Clause 14 makes this unlawful. Clause 15 gives enforcement power to the FCA with fines up to 4% of UK revenue, consistent with GDPR enforcement levels and sufficient to be material for large institutions. Clause 16 creates an independent civil remedy.

### Part 4 — Sex Worker Cooperative framework

{:start="13"}
13. Clauses 18 to 24 create the Sex Worker Cooperative. A cooperative must be owned and controlled exclusively by active worker members on a one-member-one-vote basis. Revenue must be distributed to worker members. Ancillary services — cleaning, security, bookings — may be contracted from non-members but only at market rate, independently verified. Local authorities license cooperatives against minimum welfare and safety standards in Schedule 3. Licensing is explicitly not moral enforcement: Schedule 3 Part 4 requires inspectors to be trained in working with sex workers and to conduct inspections sensitively.

### Part 5 — Anti-extraction offences

{:start="14"}
14. Clause 25 creates the core offence: receiving a financial benefit from another person's sex work outside the permitted categories. Schedule 4 sets out in detail what is and is not permitted, with worked examples. Clause 26 makes clear what is permitted: a worker keeping their own earnings, cooperative revenue sharing, genuine employment, and ancillary services at market rate. Clause 27 addresses above-market-rate ancillary service charges — the landlord charging five times market rent knowing the tenant cannot complain. Civil recovery under clause 29 is critical: it allows workers to recoup extracted earnings without criminal proceedings, and legal aid eligibility is explicitly preserved.

### Part 6 — Worker-owned content platforms

{:start="15"}
15. Clauses 31 and 32 define worker-owned content platforms and provide for certification that qualifies them for exemption from the Content Extraction Levy and for investment under the Worker-Owned Adult Content Platform Investment Programme. This Part does not create a licensing regime — it creates a definition and a certification mechanism. Clause 31(5) makes the governance and annual reporting requirements in Schedule 5 part of that definition, so a platform that does not meet them cannot be certified. The regulatory framework governing online platforms, including age verification, content standards, and illegal content obligations, is the Online Safety Act 2023, which applies to worker-owned platforms on exactly the same basis as any other platform. Clause 31(7) makes this explicit. The investment programme itself is a policy instrument established under the Sovereign Wealth Fund Act, not this Bill. This Part simply defines what qualifies.

### Part 7 — Consequential amendments and repeals

{:start="16"}
16. Clauses 33 to 39 and Schedules 6 and 7 make the necessary consequential changes. The amendments to the Sexual Offences Act 2003 sections 52 and 53 are particularly important: they make clear that participating in a licensed cooperative or providing genuine ancillary services at market rate is not an offence under the existing controlling prostitution provisions, which are not repealed and continue to apply to genuine exploitation.

### Part 8 — Interpretation, commencement and extent

{:start="17"}
17. Clause 41 staggers commencement. Decriminalisation, historical conviction disregard, and financial non-discrimination take effect on Royal Assent. The cooperative framework, the anti-extraction offences, and the worker-owned platform certification in Part 6 take effect on a day appointed by the Secretary of State, no later than 12 months after Royal Assent. Clause 42(4) clarifies that the disregard under Part 2 is automatic and the duty to delete records must be completed within 6 months of Royal Assent. Clause 43 makes clear that the Act extends to all of the United Kingdom, though the decriminalisation provisions extend to England and Wales only pending equivalent legislation in Scotland and Northern Ireland.

## Financial implications

{:start="18"}
18. The Bill has the following estimated financial implications—

- **Historical conviction disregard**: Record deletion across the Police National Computer, DBS, and biometric databases is estimated as a one-off administrative cost of £5–15 million. The ongoing benefit to individuals — through improved employment prospects, housing access, and financial inclusion — is not directly quantifiable but is significant for an estimated 30,000–100,000 people with qualifying convictions or cautions on record.
- **Income tax**: Bringing sex workers into the formal economy through financial inclusion and decriminalisation is estimated to generate £300–500 million annually in additional tax revenues, driven by increased declaration of income previously undeclared due to financial exclusion.
- **Licensing administration**: Local authorities will incur costs estimated at £40–60 million annually across England and Wales. Licence fees may recover a proportion of this cost.
- **FCA enforcement**: Estimated additional cost of £5–10 million annually in the first five years, declining as compliance improves.
- **Legal aid**: Extension to civil recovery proceedings under clause 29 is estimated at £10–20 million annually.
- **Net position**: Estimated net annual benefit of £220–420 million, with a one-off cost of £5–15 million for record deletion.

## Relationship with the Finance Act

{:start="19"}
19. The Content Extraction Levy is established by the Finance Act. This Bill defines the conditions for the worker-owned platform exemption. The two pieces of legislation work together: this Bill sets the governance standard, the Finance Act sets the fiscal consequence.

## Parliamentary procedure

{:start="20"}
20. The Bill follows standard parliamentary procedure. Provisions extending to Scotland and Northern Ireland require legislative consent motions. Secondary legislation amending primary legislation is subject to the affirmative procedure; other secondary legislation is subject to the negative procedure.
