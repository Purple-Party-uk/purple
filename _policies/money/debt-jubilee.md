## The problem

Most money in Britain is created by banks when they lend. Households then spend decades paying it back with interest. Families owe about £1.75 trillion on mortgages and £253 billion on cards, car finance, loans and overdrafts. Every month, a large share of what people earn goes to lenders, not to anything being made.

## What we would do

Pay off up to £100,000 of every adult's household debt, once. Debt on the home you live in is paid first. Whatever is left goes into your pension.

## How it works

- **Who gets it.** Everyone aged 18 or over who lives legally in the UK on the day Purple is elected. Visitors and people on student visas are not included. People seeking asylum qualify, but their money is held until their claim is decided.
- **Young people.** Children aged 12 to 17 on election day get part of the £100,000, rising with age: from about £14,000 at 12 to about £86,000 at 17. It goes into their pension pot.
- **What gets paid.** Your mortgage or rent-to-buy debt first, then your other debts, highest interest first. Council tax, energy, water and rent arrears count. Debts you owe the government are written off separately.
- **What does not.** Buy-to-let mortgages, business loans, and loans between friends or family.
- **Which balance.** What you owed on election day, or on the day of payment if that is lower. New borrowing after election day does not count.
- **Couples.** Your £100,000 is yours. It can clear your own debts and debts you share, but not your partner's own debts. If it pays more towards a shared home, you own more of the home.
- **What is left.** It goes into your Sovereign Wealth Fund pension pot as Jubilee Bonds: government bonds that cannot be sold and that grow faster than prices.
- **Taking it out.** From 60, you can take up to one-twentieth of what is left each year. Because it is a share of what is left, it never runs out. You can also use it at any time to pay for your rent-to-buy home, or give it to close family.
- **When you die.** What is left passes to your family's pension pots.
- **Landlords.** When your Jubilee pays for your rent-to-buy home, your landlord's mortgage is paid off. The rest goes into the landlord's pension pot, not their pocket.
- **Mortgage payments.** If your mortgage or rent-to-buy debt is cleared, you pay half your old monthly payment into your own pension pot for three years. The money is still yours.
- **Student debt.** Written off in full, on top of the £100,000.
- **When.** In year two, after a year of setting up.

## What it costs

About £6 trillion in one go. That is roughly twice the size of the economy, and we say so plainly. The government creates this money; it does not borrow it from markets. Most of it ends up as bonds in people's own pension pots, which they draw on slowly over their lifetime. The yearly costs are the return on those bonds, what people over 60 take out, and running the scheme. [CHECK: final costing before publication.]

## Who it affects

- **Everyone 18 or over:** the same £100,000, as debt cleared or pension savings.
- **Renters:** most start rent-to-buy with a large share of their home already paid for.
- **People over 60:** a steady extra income on top of the £18,000 income floor.
- **Low earners:** a flat sum is worth far more to them than to high earners.
- **Landlords:** paid full value for the share their tenants buy, but as pension savings, not cash.
- **Lenders:** repaid what they are owed, with no fees or penalties.
