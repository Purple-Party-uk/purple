## The problem

Gains on shares are only taxed when they are sold. A person with a large portfolio can hold it for decades, let it grow, and never sell. Their wealth rises every year while their tax bill stays close to nothing.

## What we would do

We would charge a yearly levy on holdings of shares and bonds listed on a stock exchange.

| Value of listed shares and bonds | Yearly levy on that part |
|---|---|
| Up to £1 million | 0% |
| £1 million – £10 million | 1% |
| Over £10 million | 2% |

It covers all shares listed in the UK, whoever owns them, and all listed shares and bonds held anywhere in the world by people who live in the UK for tax purposes.

## How it works

The levy is collected automatically by the banks and brokers that hold the shares, so most people never fill in a form.

Each rate applies only to the part inside that band. Someone with £3 million in shares pays nothing on the first £1 million and 1% on the next £2 million: £20,000 a year.

UK residents must declare all their listed holdings, wherever they are held. Countries already share account information with each other under an international agreement, the [Common Reporting Standard](https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/common-reporting-standard/), which does most of the checking.

Anyone who leaves the UK to live elsewhere for tax purposes pays a one-off levy on their whole portfolio, so moving abroad is not a way out.

**A register of large assets.** Anything worth more than £50,000 that is not a listed share, such as shares in a private company, cryptocurrency, art or classic cars, must be registered within 30 days of buying it. There is no yearly tax on these. The register simply records what people own, so that gains and gifts can be measured fairly later. Hiding an asset is a criminal offence.

## What it costs

We estimate the levy would raise £50–65 billion a year.

## Who it affects

**Almost everyone** pays nothing. The levy starts only above £1 million in listed shares and bonds.

**People with very large share portfolios** pay a small part of their wealth each year, whether or not they sell.
