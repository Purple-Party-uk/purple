## The problem

Council Tax in England is still based on what homes were worth in [April 1991](https://www.gov.uk/council-tax-bands). The top band pays only three times as much as the bottom band, so a mansion worth tens of millions can pay little more than a family semi.

Stamp Duty is a tax on moving. It makes people pay a large sum at the moment they can least afford it, and it puts people off moving to a home that suits them better.

## What we would do

We would abolish Council Tax and Stamp Duty. In their place, every home would pay a yearly tax based on what it is worth now, land and building together.

| Value of the home | Rate on that part |
|---|---|
| Up to £500,000 | 0.6% |
| £500,000 – £1 million | 0.8% |
| £1 million – £2 million | 1.2% |
| £2 million – £5 million | 1.8% |
| Over £5 million | 2.5% |
| Second homes and holiday homes | 3.5% of the whole value |
| Homes left empty | 3.5% of the whole value |

UK property owned through an offshore company whose real owner is not on the UK register would pay an extra 2% a year.

## How it works

Like income tax, each rate applies only to the part of the value inside that band.

- A £300,000 home pays £1,800 a year.
- A £750,000 home pays £5,000 a year.
- A £3 million home pays £37,000 a year.

Homes are valued every year using Land Registry sales, local price trends and satellite images. If you think your valuation is more than 10% out, you can challenge it with an independent valuation. If you are right, the state pays for that valuation.

The bill on the home you live in cannot rise by more than 5% in any year, whatever happens to prices.

**If you own your home but have a low income**, you can put off paying. This applies to anyone living in their only home whose property tax is more than a tenth of their income, such as a pensioner who bought decades ago. The unpaid tax builds up against the home, with interest at the rate the government pays to borrow, and is paid when the home is sold or from the estate. You pay the same tax in the end, just later. Once the amount owed reaches 80% of the home's value, you pay from income or sell.

There are no exemptions. Households on the guaranteed income floor have the tax covered by that payment.

## What it costs

We estimate the new property tax would raise £43–52 billion a year, and the offshore surcharge another £2–3 billion. Today, Council Tax raises about £45 billion and Stamp Duty about £15 billion.

## Who it affects

**People buying or selling a home** pay no Stamp Duty.

**People in the most expensive homes** pay much more than they do now, because their bill finally reflects what their home is worth.

**Owners of second homes, holiday homes and empty homes** pay a higher flat rate, so leaving a home empty no longer pays.

**Older owners on modest incomes** can put the tax off until the home is sold, so nobody is forced out of their home by the bill.
