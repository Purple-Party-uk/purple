## The problem

VAT adds 20% to the price of most things you buy, from clothes and phones to repairs and haircuts. Everyone pays the same rate, whatever they earn. That means VAT takes a bigger share of a low income than a high one, because people on low incomes spend almost everything they have.

## What we would do

We would take VAT off all standard goods and services.

VAT would stay only on luxuries and on things that cause harm.

**Luxuries, at 30%:** private jets and helicopters, yachts, jewellery and watches over £1,000, designer fashion over £500 from named luxury brands, cars over £60,000, private cosmetic surgery, fine art over £10,000, private members' clubs costing more than £5,000 a year, first and business class flights, hotel rooms over £300 a night, and furs.

| Things that cause harm | Rate |
|---|---|
| Gambling and payday loans | 30% |
| Tobacco | 25% |
| Alcohol | 20% |
| Intoxicating substances | 15% |
| Economy flights | 15% |

The 15% on economy flights replaces Air Passenger Duty.

## How it works

Shops and firms would be required by law to pass the cut on in lower prices, so the saving reaches the customer rather than the company.

Some other charges change too:

- The TV licence fee is abolished. The BBC is paid for from general taxation.
- Insurance Premium Tax rises to 15%.
- Fuel duty and car tax stay as they are.

## What it costs

Taking VAT off standard goods gives up about £140 billion a year. VAT on luxuries and harmful goods would still raise an estimated £29.6 billion a year.

The difference is paid for by the new income tax and the other changes in [Your Money & Security]({{ '/policies/money/' | relative_url }}).

## Who it affects

**Everyone** pays less for everyday shopping. The saving is largest, as a share of income, for people on low and middle incomes.

**Households** stop paying the TV licence fee.

**People who buy luxury goods, gamble, smoke, drink or fly** pay VAT on those things, at higher rates than today in some cases.

**People who buy insurance** pay more tax on their premiums.
