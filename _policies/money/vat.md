## The problem

VAT adds 20% to the price of most things you buy, from clothes and phone bills to petrol, repairs and haircuts. Everyone pays the same rate, whatever they earn. That means VAT takes a bigger share of a low income than a high one, because people on low incomes spend almost everything they have.

It costs businesses too. Every firm above the threshold has to register, charge VAT, keep the records and file a return every quarter. That is time spent on paperwork instead of on customers.

## What we would do

We would take everyday goods and services out of VAT altogether. Not a 0% rate: out of the system entirely.

VAT would stay only on two kinds of things.

**Luxuries, at 30%:** private jets and helicopters, yachts, jewellery and watches over £1,000, designer fashion over £500 from named luxury brands, cars over £60,000, private cosmetic surgery, fine art over £10,000, private members' clubs costing more than £5,000 a year, first and business class flights, hotel rooms over £300 a night, and furs.

**Things with a social or environmental cost:**

| What | Rate |
|---|---|
| Gambling and payday loans | 30% |
| Tobacco | 25% of the recommended retail price |
| Economy flights | 15% |
| Intoxicating substances | 15% |
| Alcohol | Taxed through a higher duty on each unit of alcohol instead of VAT |

The 15% on economy flights replaces Air Passenger Duty.

**A holiday should not be a luxury.** Households on lower incomes get back the VAT on economy flights for one holiday a year. You don't fill in a form: the airline reports the booking, and HMRC pays the money back automatically. A household qualifies if its income is below:

| Household | Income below |
|---|---|
| Single adult | £20,000 |
| Couple | £30,000 |
| Single parent with one child | £26,000 |
| Couple with two children | £42,000 |

Each extra adult adds £10,000 and each child adds £6,000. [CHECK: proposed figures, to be confirmed in the costing]

## How it works

**Most businesses stop collecting VAT.** Unless you sell luxury goods, gambling, payday loans or flights, you no longer register, charge VAT or file returns.

**Pubs and corner shops are spared too.** VAT on tobacco and intoxicating substances, and the duty on alcohol, are paid by the producer or importer, at the same point duty is paid today. The price the shop or pub pays already includes it. They never register.

**Wholesalers only sell to businesses.** A firm that sells luxury goods only to other businesses, at trade prices, does not register. As soon as it sells to the public, it charges VAT on those sales, at the price the customer pays.

**No threshold.** Any business selling the goods above charges VAT, however small. Otherwise a small seller could undercut a larger one by 20–30% on exactly the things the higher rates exist to price.

**No claiming VAT back.** Today, registered businesses reclaim the VAT they pay. Under Purple, VAT is charged once, on the final sale. A business that buys a luxury for its own use, such as a business-class flight, pays the same as anyone else.

**The cut reaches your shopping bill.** Shops and firms will be required by law to pass the cut on in lower prices, so the saving goes to the customer, not the company.

### Insurance

Insurance Premium Tax comes off the insurance most people need:

- each adult's first car, including breakdown cover;
- buildings and contents insurance on your home;
- pet insurance;
- travel insurance.

Life insurance and income protection stay untaxed, as they are today. Other insurance pays 15%, including second cars, second homes, private medical insurance, gadget cover and extended warranties.

Fuel duty and car tax stay as they are.

## What it costs

Taking VAT off everyday goods gives up about £140 billion a year. VAT on luxuries and on things with a social or environmental cost would still raise an estimated £29.6 billion a year.

The difference is paid for by the new income tax and the other changes in [Your Money & Security]({{ '/policies/money/' | relative_url }}).

## Who it affects

**Everyone** pays less for everyday shopping. The saving is largest, as a share of income, for people on low and middle incomes.

**Most businesses** stop registering for VAT and filing returns.

**Households on lower incomes** get the flight VAT back on one holiday a year.

**Drivers and homeowners** stop paying tax on their car and home insurance.

**People who buy luxury goods, gamble, smoke, drink or fly** pay more on those things, at higher rates than today in some cases.
