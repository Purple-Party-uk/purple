## The problem

Wealth in Britain is taxed far more lightly than work, and much of it is barely taxed at all.

Gains on shares are only taxed when they are sold. A person with a large portfolio can hold it for decades, let it grow, and never sell. Their wealth rises every year while their tax bill stays close to nothing.

The wealthiest also have ways to keep money out of their own name. They hold it in a trust or a private company, and take the benefit instead of an income: a house to live in, a yacht to use, or a loan that never gets repaid. On paper they earn little. In practice they live very well.

## What we would do

We would make large fortunes contribute every year, and close the routes used to hide them. There are four parts.

1. A yearly levy on large holdings of shares and bonds.
2. A register of valuable assets.
3. Trusts taxed as if the money went straight to the person who benefits.
4. Perks and loans from your own company or trust taxed as income.

We would not charge a yearly tax on everything people own. Your home is covered by the [fair property tax]({{ '/policies/money/property-tax/' | relative_url }}), and ordinary savings are not touched.

## How it works

### Levy on large share portfolios

Shares and bonds listed on a stock exchange pay a yearly levy above £1 million.

| Value of listed shares and bonds | Yearly levy on that part |
|---|---|
| Up to £1 million | 0% |
| £1 million – £10 million | 1% |
| Over £10 million | 2% |

Each rate applies only to the part inside that band. Someone with £3 million in shares pays nothing on the first £1 million and 1% on the next £2 million, which comes to £20,000 a year.

It covers all shares listed in the UK, whoever owns them, and all listed shares and bonds held anywhere in the world by people who live in the UK for tax purposes. The banks and brokers that hold the shares collect it automatically.

UK residents must declare all their listed holdings, wherever they are held. Countries already share account information under an international agreement, the [Common Reporting Standard](https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/common-reporting-standard/), which does most of the checking.

Anyone who leaves the UK to live elsewhere for tax purposes pays a one-off levy on their whole portfolio, so moving abroad is not a way out.

### A register of valuable assets

Anything worth more than £50,000 that is not a listed share, such as shares in a private company, cryptocurrency, art, wine or classic cars, must be registered within 30 days of buying it. Its value is updated each year.

There is no yearly tax on these assets. The register records what people own, so that gains, gifts and perks can be measured fairly later. Hiding an asset is a criminal offence.

### Trusts

Today trusts have their own tax rules, with charges every ten years and on certain transfers. We would scrap those special rules.

Instead, we would look through the trust to the people it pays. Money or benefits paid out of a trust are taxed as if they had been paid straight to the person who receives them. A loan from a trust counts as that person's borrowing under the loan rules below.

Charities and employee-owned firms are not affected.

### Perks from your own company or trust

If a company, trust or partnership you control or are connected to lets you have something for less than it is worth, the difference is taxed as your income. That could be living in its house, using its yacht or flying on its jet.

Pay the full market rent and there is nothing to tax. Pay half, and the other half is taxed. Anything passed between people or firms that are connected is treated as if it were sold at its full value, whatever price was actually paid.

Genuine work items, such as a laptop, an expense account or a work car, are on a fixed list and are not taxed.

### Living on loans instead of income

Some very wealthy people borrow against their shares or property, live on the loan, and leave the debt to be repaid from their estate. This is sometimes called "buy, borrow, die". Under our rules, borrowing used to live on instead of income is taxed as income.

Ordinary borrowing is untouched. That includes your mortgage, student loan, business borrowing, and up to £100,000 of other personal borrowing such as car finance and credit cards.

Above that, a loan counts as a real loan only if it is repaid within seven years, at a proper interest rate, from income that has already been taxed. A loan from your own company or trust has to be repaid within 12 months. If not, the money is taxed as income in that year.

Lenders report loans over £20,000 to HMRC, so the rule can be checked without anyone having to declare their total wealth.

## What it costs

We estimate the portfolio levy would raise £50–65 billion a year.

The other rules raise money by stopping wealth slipping out of income tax and the [gifts and inheritance]({{ '/policies/money/inheritance/' | relative_url }}) allowance. We have not put a separate figure on them.

## Who it affects

**Almost everyone** pays nothing. The levy starts only above £1 million in listed shares and bonds, and ordinary borrowing and work perks are not touched.

**People with very large share portfolios** pay a small part of their wealth each year, whether or not they sell.

**People who use trusts, private companies or large loans to avoid tax** find that the benefit they get is taxed as income, like everyone else's.
