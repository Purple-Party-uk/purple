## The problem

Every government since 2010 has promised to cut net migration to a target. Every one of them has missed it.

They missed it because most people come to Britain for one reason: employers want their work. When the economy needs workers, people come. Border rules decide who comes and on what terms. They do not decide how many.

Tougher borders often make the numbers go up. People who fear they will not be allowed back stop moving back and forth. They stay for good and bring their families. Every announced crackdown sets off a rush to get in first.

Meanwhile, the system we have is expensive and unfair. Workers tied to one employer cannot leave a bad job. Families pay thousands of pounds in fees for each renewal. And some employers use cheap, insecure migrant labour to hold down everyone's wages.

## What we would do

We would control migration where control actually works: through wages, working conditions and enforcement against employers who break the rules.

- **Work visas that belong to the worker.** Equal rights from the day you arrive, no upfront fee, and freedom to change jobs.
- **Employers pay, not workers.** A levy on employers who recruit from abroad, and the right to recruit only in sectors that meet fair pay and conditions.
- **A clear path to citizenship.** Settlement after five years and citizenship after seven and a half, with no fee.
- **Enforcement in workplaces**, not shows of force at the border.
- **No net migration target.** Honest figures instead, published every year.

We will be honest about one thing. Our plans for growth, including a large housebuilding programme and more public investment in research, will increase the demand for workers. A growing economy draws people in. No party controls the total. What a government can control is who comes, on what terms, and who benefits.

## How it works

### Four visas

There would be four routes: a work visa, a working holiday visa for young people, a student visa and a retiree visa.

Holders of the last three cannot claim public funds. They pay a small monthly health contribution straight to the NHS, which replaces today's Immigration Health Surcharge. Work visa holders pay no health contribution.

### Work visas

Each year the Migration Advisory Committee estimates where the labour shortages are, sector by sector. Shortage occupations score more points. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have a formal say on targets for their own labour markets. There is no income threshold.

A sector can recruit from abroad only if it meets the wage floors and working conditions agreed through sector-wide bargaining under our Work policy. A sector that relies on migrants because its jobs are badly paid has to fix the jobs.

The visa belongs to the worker. They can change jobs without reapplying. A worker who can walk away from a bad employer is much harder to exploit.

From the day they arrive, work visa holders have the same rights as anyone else: the NHS, the [£18,000 income floor]({{ '/policies/money/nit/' | relative_url }}) and full employment protection.

### The employer levy

Employers pay a levy for each work visa holder during that worker's first five years. It replaces the Immigration Skills Charge.

- The levy is 2.5% of the worker's salary, with a minimum of £1,320 a year. The minimum is halved for small employers.
- The minimum bites hardest on low-paid jobs. That is deliberate. Where pay is low, we want employers to raise it rather than recruit from abroad.
- Employers cannot take the levy out of the worker's pay.
- Public bodies such as the NHS and councils are exempt, because our public sector pay reform raises pay in those jobs. Private firms delivering public services, such as care homes paid by councils, still pay.
- The money goes on training in the same sector and on enforcing employment standards.

### What a visa costs the worker

There is no upfront fee and there are no renewal fees. Instead, each work visa holder pays a small surcharge on their UK earnings for five years. That one surcharge covers everything, from the first visa to citizenship.

The independent Fiscal Council sets the rate each year so that it covers the cost of processing visas, settlement and citizenship, and no more. The law caps it at 1% of earnings. Children pay nothing. Nobody pays it on income-floor payments, so it never pushes anyone below £18,000. Refugees do not pay it.

Decisions on visas would be made within four weeks.

### Coming and going

All visas allow people to leave and come back as often as they like. Some time spent abroad counts towards settlement, and people with settled status keep it during longer absences than they can today. People who know they can come back are more likely to leave when the work runs out.

### Settlement and citizenship

- **Settlement** after five years on a work visa.
- **Citizenship** after seven and a half years. Applicants must speak English. There is no civic test and no fee. Citizenship is not for sale.
- **Children** who have lived here for seven and a half years become British automatically, with no application and no fee, whatever their parents' status.
- **Local elections.** After two years living here, people can vote for their council. This does not include general elections.

### Family

A British citizen can bring a foreign husband, wife or partner to the UK without an income test.

A parent without status whose child is British can stay and work while the child is under 18, unless the parent has a serious criminal conviction. Years spent working or caring count towards settlement, so most parents will qualify to stay in their own right before the child turns 18.

Parents and grandparents of British citizens and settled residents can visit for up to three months a year without a visa. They need a free online travel authorisation and health insurance. Emergency care is free for everyone until they are stable, so nobody hesitates to call an ambulance.

### Enforcement in workplaces, not on beaches

Most people without legal status did not arrive in a small boat. They came on a visa and stayed after it ran out, and most of them are working. So we would move money from border measures that exist for show into workplace inspection.

The Employment Standards Agency would go after employers who use undocumented workers or break wage and working-time rules. Directors who knowingly and systematically use illegal labour would face prosecution.

There would be no immigration checks in schools, GP surgeries or hospitals, and no workplace raids aimed at workers. Nobody should keep a child out of school or avoid a doctor because they are afraid.

### Counting honestly

We would scrap the net migration target. Each year the Migration Advisory Committee would publish a forecast and, on the same day, the actual figures for the year before. Full entry and exit checks would show who has overstayed a visa. They would not count small boat arrivals or journeys to and from Ireland, and we will not pretend they do.

### Integration

A national Integration Service would support migrants and refugees for up to five years after arrival, with free English classes and help getting qualifications recognised, finding work and registering with services.

## What it costs

We have not yet put figures on this policy. These are the costs and income it carries.

- **The visa surcharge** is set to cover the cost of processing, from first visa to citizenship. It is not designed to raise money, and it replaces the income from today's fees.
- **The employer levy** raises new money, earmarked for training and for enforcing employment standards.
- **Equal rights for work visa holders** cost money in services and income-floor payments, set against the tax they pay.
- **The Integration Service** is a new cost.

We will publish figures once these have been modelled.

## Who it affects

**People who come here to work** get equal rights, no upfront fees and the freedom to leave a bad employer. They pay a small share of their earnings for five years instead.

**Workers already here** are protected, because employers can no longer use migrant labour to undercut wages and conditions.

**Employers** that rely on low pay face higher costs through the levy and sector-wide standards. That is intended. Employers who already treat their staff fairly gain, because competitors who undercut them get caught.

**Families** can bring a partner without an income test, and children who grow up here become British.
