## The problem

Britain has got faster at making first decisions on asylum claims. In June 2026, about 40,000 people were waiting for one, half as many as a year earlier. The number of asylum seekers in hotels also halved, to about 16,000.

The delay has moved to the appeals stage. By the middle of 2026, more than 90,000 asylum appeals were waiting to be heard, more than double the number at the end of 2024. Appeals were taking well over a year to decide. While people wait, most cannot work, and many stay in hotels at public expense.

Refugee charities say poor first decisions are forcing people into appeals. Speeding up the Home Office without fixing the quality of its decisions moves the queue to the courts instead of clearing it.

There are still almost no safe, legal ways to claim asylum from abroad. So people pay smugglers. Governments respond with tougher rules and plans to send people to other countries. Neither has stopped the boats.

Removal is often blocked for reasons that have nothing to do with the person's claim. Some countries refuse to take their own citizens back. Some people wanted for crimes at home argue that their country's prisons would breach their human rights. They end up stuck here for years, in limbo.

## What we would do

- **A decision on every claim within six months**, and appeals heard within three to twelve months, with more caseworkers, legal aid and specialist tribunals.
- **Safe legal routes** to apply from abroad, so that fewer people need smugglers.
- **Reception centres instead of hotels**, with the right to work from day one.
- **No removals to third countries.** People whose claims fail go back to their own country.
- **Pressure on countries that refuse to take people back**, and treaties so that people wanted for crimes abroad can be removed.
- **Clear rules on deportation**, so that family life stops working as an automatic bar to removing serious offenders.

## How it works

### Who can claim

Countries would be graded green, amber or red. People from green countries cannot claim asylum. People from amber countries can claim in specific circumstances. Claims from red countries are fast-tracked. The system is designed to comply with the European Convention on Human Rights.

People could apply from abroad, online or at UK-run centres overseas, and travel once accepted. A safe legal route takes business away from the smugglers.

A work visa holder cannot claim asylum when their visa ends unless conditions in their home country have genuinely changed.

### Six months to decide

Every claim would be decided within six months, phased in over three years. That means three to four times as many Home Office caseworkers and legal aid for asylum claims.

Appeals would be heard by a legally qualified judge and two specialist members, one an expert in immigration law and one in the country concerned. Hearings would take place in regional centres, not only in London. Simple cases would be decided within three months and complex ones within a year. Better, up-to-date information about conditions in each country would mean fewer wrong decisions and fewer appeals.

### While a claim is decided

People can work from day one. They live in regional reception centres, not hotels, with English classes, legal advice and health checks. Support to settle in starts on arrival and carries on after a decision.

People arriving by small boat are held briefly for identity, health and security checks, then move to a reception centre and report regularly. Holding everyone for the whole of their claim does not stop the crossings.

Children are never detained. Families with children live at a fixed address and report regularly. Children who arrive alone are cared for through a national fostering service.

The number of places in detention is set in law and checked every quarter by an independent inspectorate. If it is exceeded, people are released and must report.

### Refugees who travel home

A refugee who goes back to the country they fled, without permission, shows that it is safe for them. Refugees would need Home Office approval before travelling there. Urgent requests, such as a dying relative, are decided quickly. The Home Office can also approve short visits so refugees can judge whether it is safe to go home for good.

Unauthorised travel triggers a review of their refugee status. The refugee gets the evidence against them, legal aid, an interpreter and a right of appeal. They can keep their status if they show, with independent evidence, that they were forced to go, that a genuine emergency left no time to ask, or that they did not rely on their home country's protection. A partner or children with their own status keep it.

### When someone cannot be returned

If return would expose someone to war, persecution or torture, they get Humanitarian Protection, not years in limbo.

If return is safe but blocked because their country will not take them back, Britain puts escalating pressure on that country, through visas, aid, trade and diplomacy. The person lives under restrictions for up to two years. If their country still refuses and they have committed no crime, they get limited leave to remain.

### People fleeing prosecution

People wanted for ordinary crimes at home are not refugees, and they are not entitled to Humanitarian Protection. Asylum claims would be checked against Interpol notices and extradition requests.

Where poor prison conditions block their removal, we would negotiate treaties so that their sentence is served in a UK prison and they are removed once it is served. Where a country prefers to hold its own nationals, Britain could fund and monitor prison units that meet UK standards.

These treaties will take years. Until they exist, some of these people will stay here, under the strictest conditions the law allows, including electronic tags. We will not promise removals we cannot yet deliver.

### Deportation and family life

Anyone sentenced to a year or more in prison is deported, unless removal would do severe, lasting harm to a child or dependant that nothing else could prevent. People with shorter sentences, and people who offend repeatedly, are assessed case by case, and repeat offending counts heavily towards removal.

A family formed while someone was here unlawfully, or while their claim was pending, carries little weight. Nobody is ever returned to torture or inhuman treatment, whatever their record.

An offence committed as a child never leads to deportation. Children are dealt with through the youth justice system, not the immigration system.

### Fraud

Anyone who got a visa or status by fraud is removed where possible and barred from applying for any UK visa for at least 20 years.

## What it costs

We have not yet put figures on this policy. These are the costs it carries.

- **Faster decisions** need three to four times as many caseworkers, legal aid for asylum claims and more tribunal capacity.
- **Reception centres** replace hotels.
- **Treaties and prison units** abroad carry negotiation and monitoring costs.

Against these, a system that decides claims in months rather than years should cut today's spending on hotels and long-running cases. We will publish figures once these have been modelled.

## Who it affects

**People seeking asylum** get a decision within six months, the right to work while they wait and a reception centre instead of a hotel.

**Refugees** keep their protection for as long as they need it, and can plan to go home safely when conditions allow.

**People whose claims fail** are returned to their own country, not to a third country.

**Communities** where asylum hotels are now housed see them close.

**Serious offenders without British citizenship** face deportation under clear rules, except where return would mean torture or inhuman treatment.
