Governments keep promising to cut immigration to a number, and they keep missing it. Most people come to Britain because employers want their work. Border rules can decide who comes and on what terms. They cannot set the total, and tougher rules often push it up.

We think the honest answer is to control migration where control actually works. That means fair pay and conditions in every sector, so that employers cannot use migrant workers to undercut everyone else. It means enforcement in workplaces, where most people without legal status are working, rather than shows of force at the border.

People who come here to work should have the same rights as everyone else from the day they arrive, and a fast, cheap and secure route to settle. People fleeing danger should get a fair decision within six months.

We would also give legal status to people who already live here without it. Many have worked and raised children here for years. Removing them one by one would cost more than the state could ever recover, and leaving them as they are keeps them outside the tax system and open to exploitation. So we would run a single, one-off regularisation scheme for people who can show they have built a life here. Anyone convicted of a serious crime, or who used fraud, would be excluded. Once the scheme closes, the ordinary rules apply to everyone.

None of this will bring the numbers down to a target. A growing economy draws people in. What we can promise is clear rules, applied consistently, and honest figures every year.
